# Ether Frontier — Heroes: Velimudr and Tara Character and Visual Direction v1

Status: **canonical character and visual direction; approved 2026-07-27**  
Updated: 2026-07-27

## Approved scope

This document canonically defines only:

- the source position of Velimudr and Tara;
- their adapted origins and character conflicts;
- their in-world appearance;
- their static gameplay silhouettes.

It does not approve passive abilities, unique buildings, animations, numerical
balance, prompts or generated art.

Spas remains unchanged and canonical.

# Part I — Velimudr

## 1. Source position

In the pseudo-historical corpus, **The Word of Wisdom of Volkhv Velimudr** is
presented as the sayings of an ancient sage of Belovodye. Its advocates claim
that the sayings survived in several scripts and physical forms: wooden boards,
parchment copies and later transcriptions.

This unstable transmission makes Velimudr valuable for the game's satire. He
can represent the difference between a person's words and the authoritative
text later assembled in that person's name.

## 2. Adapted identity

**Velimudr is a personal name, but not necessarily the author's original
name.**

It means “the one credited with great wisdom” in later editions. The man
himself never uses it. In his surviving notebooks he signs only with a small
index mark meaning “speaker present”.

He lives in Belovodye many generations after the Daariyan migration. He is a
teacher, public arbitrator and keeper of incompatible survivor accounts.

Velimudr does not claim to possess the complete ancient wisdom. His teaching
method is built around comparing statements, identifying their speaker and
leaving unresolved questions visible.

## 3. Origin

Velimudr was born beside one of the rebuilt southern water routes. His family
maintained a public resting house where travellers exchanged news, songs and
copies of damaged records.

He learned early that one event may arrive in several forms:

- a witness remembers what happened;
- a singer remembers what it meant;
- an administrator remembers what was permitted;
- a copied board remembers the wording of an earlier copy;
- a ruin remembers physical consequences without explaining them.

He became a volkhv not as a priest performing miracles but as a professional
keeper of speech, judgement and attributed memory.

## 4. Dramatic role

Students begin recording Velimudr's conversations. After his death is falsely
announced during a long journey, editors publish the notes as one coherent
**Word of Wisdom**.

The published Velimudr is calm, certain and never contradicts himself. The
living Velimudr is furious to discover that all his doubts, revisions and
questions have been removed.

His campaign conflict is not proving that the book is entirely false. Most of
its sentences are genuinely his. The falsehood is the impression that he said
them once, in that order, with one final meaning.

## 5. Character

### Outward behaviour

- listens to a complete answer before asking who first recorded it;
- repeats an opponent's claim more clearly than the opponent did;
- distinguishes lying, misremembering and copying;
- refuses ceremonial seating above other speakers;
- annotates his own mistakes in a different colour;
- becomes visibly impatient with reverence directed at him.

### Inner contradiction

Velimudr values the right to revise a statement, but secretly wants later
generations to recognise that his own revisions were especially wise.

He attacks the cult of his authority while enjoying the influence it gives him.
He must decide whether to surrender control of his words completely or become
the final authorised editor he claims to oppose.

### Fear

His deepest fear is not being misquoted. It is discovering that the simplified
version of his teaching genuinely helps more people than the difficult original.

### Dry humour

> “Master, which copy contains your true words?”  
> “The one in which I ask you not to call me Master.”  
> “That sentence is absent from all copies.”  
> “Then your catalogue is improving.”

## 6. Age and physique

- apparent age: 66–72;
- medium height, narrow but not frail;
- slightly stooped from working over low public tables;
- long forearms and strong hands stained with different inks and metal oxides;
- walks with a measured uneven rhythm caused by an old river-crossing injury;
- capable of travel but clearly not a warrior.

He should contrast with Spas's broad surveyor physique.

## 7. Face

- triangular face with high cheekbones and a narrow jaw;
- deep horizontal forehead lines from concentration rather than anger;
- dark grey eyes that continually shift between speaker and record;
- silver-grey hair cut to shoulder length and tied with a plain cloth strip;
- narrow forked beard with one side shorter after a fire accident;
- small ink marks on two fingers frequently transferred to his cheek;
- no glowing gaze, prophetic blindness or idealised elder beauty.

## 8. Clothing

Velimudr belongs to Belovodye, a civilisation built from surviving and rebuilt
layers rather than Daariya's monumental uniformity.

### Base coat

A long warm-grey coat assembled from several high-quality repaired fabrics.
Every repair is dated by a small material change, not written decoration.

### Speaker's shoulder cloth

One asymmetric deep-indigo cloth lies across the right shoulder and unfolds
into a working surface. Its edge contains empty attachment holes where earlier
owners removed disputed statements.

### Revision apron

A muted burgundy waist apron holds separate writing plates, binding clips and
colour samples. It is practical and slightly overfilled.

### Footwear

Soft dark boots with one reinforced left sole. No ceremonial sandals or
wandering-monk costume.

## 9. Equipment

### Table of Three Versions

Velimudr carries a folding triangular reading table. Each side holds a different
version of the same short passage:

- damaged early copy;
- confident public edition;
- Velimudr's later correction.

The table never forms a perfect triangle because the three hinge lengths differ.
It is his defining visual object.

### Speaker-present token

A small ivory token with an open centre identifies testimony given in the
speaker's presence. It is deliberately plain and must not resemble a magical
seal.

### Revision clips

Large copper and blackened-steel clips sit along his left forearm. Each has a
different jaw shape for attaching, disputing or withdrawing a statement.

### Unfinished board

One blank-edged wooden board hangs behind the belt. Velimudr refuses to fill its
missing final line merely to complete the composition.

A narrow replaceable attack plate is secured to the board's empty edge. It has
several mismatched physical notches, an open starting bracket and a visible
unfinished break. A thin owner thread returns its perforated pieces after
Velimudr's approved Spoken Word attack. The plate carries no readable sacred
script and does not alter his primary static silhouette.

## 10. Palette

- dominant: warm stone grey;
- identity field: deep indigo;
- revision accent: muted burgundy;
- paper and board: ivory, birch cream and aged brown;
- clips: oxidised copper and charcoal metal;
- small correction accents: mineral blue and dull red;
- no white-gold priest palette.

## 11. Gameplay-object silhouette

In exact top-down cardboard representation, Velimudr is recognised by:

1. asymmetric indigo shoulder cloth extending to the right;
2. folded triangular three-version table on the left;
3. long narrow body silhouette;
4. row of oversized revision clips along the left forearm;
5. unfinished rectangular board behind the belt.

The triangle, rectangle and open-centre token create his visual language. He
must not resemble Spas's circles and stone mantle.

## 12. Static pose for first concept

Velimudr stands beside a monumental Belovodye wall displaying an enormous
official quotation attributed to him. His triangular table is open below it,
showing three incompatible versions.

He is reaching upward with a revision clip, not to destroy the inscription but
to attach the missing source and date beneath it.

Several listeners continue copying the wall while he works.

## 13. Explicit exclusions

- no wizard, druid, monk or generic sage costume;
- no staff, floating book, magic ink or glowing letters;
- no pseudo-runes or readable imitation sacred script;
- no enormous beard covering the body silhouette;
- no infallible teacher or purely cynical fraud;
- no direct visual borrowing from a real religious leader;
- no Daariyan stone mantle or Three-Moon Measure;
- no ability effects or animation frames at this stage.

# Part II — Tara

## 14. Source position

In the later pseudo-historical tradition, Tara is described as the daughter of
Perun, younger sister of Tarkh Dazhbog and guardian of Belovodye. Alternative
etymology then presents Tartaria as the land of Tarkh and Tara.

The game preserves Tara as a central figure of post-flood restoration but makes
the claimed divine genealogy and place-name explanation part of her later
legend, not unquestioned narration.

## 15. Adapted identity

**Tara is a title meaning Keeper of Returned Ground.**

The title is granted after the Daariyan flood to the person responsible for
deciding when drowned, frozen or contaminated land may be inhabited again.

The playable Tara is the first holder of the title. Her birth name is lost
because later genealogies replace it with a divine identity.

She is not literally Dazhbog's biological sister in the secure historical
layer. She worked under the same Solar Covenant and publicly opposed several
of his post-catastrophe decisions. Later priests convert this political kinship
into a heavenly family.

## 16. Origin

Tara was born in a Rasen climate settlement south of Daariya. Her community
received the first mixed columns of polar refugees:

- all four fleet traditions;
- Naga water-route keepers;
- Arey underground survivors;
- people carrying copied or incomplete bodies;
- livestock and seed ecologies rescued from lunar flooding.

She began as a soil and water assessor. Her task was to decide not who deserved
the land, but whether the land could survive another settlement.

Tara earns her title after refusing to open a fertile valley until displaced
Lower Passage communities are included in its water covenant. The delay saves
the valley from a second pressure collapse.

## 17. Dramatic role

Tara becomes the public face of Belovodye's recovery. Settlements call
themselves “lands returned by Tara”. Several generations later, editors shorten
this to “lands of Tara” and then join it with Tarkh's name to manufacture the
etymology of a universal northern empire.

Her genuine achievements are therefore used to support a claim she never made.

Her story asks whether restoration creates ownership. Tara can repair ground,
but she cannot decide that everyone living upon it now belongs to one culture.

## 18. Character

### Outward behaviour

- touches water before soil and soil before plans;
- speaks directly, with little patience for ceremonial genealogy;
- remembers which settlement owes water or seed to another;
- refuses to call damaged land empty;
- celebrates visible repair rather than untouched purity;
- treats a scar as evidence of survival, not contamination.

### Inner contradiction

Tara understands ecosystems as relationships, yet often treats communities as
components she may arrange for collective survival.

Like Spas, she is capable of deciding for others, but for a different reason:
Spas trusts the forecast; Tara trusts the system she has successfully repaired.

She must learn that a stable settlement can still be unjust.

### Fear

Her deepest fear is that the recovered land will fail after people trust her
judgement. This makes her overprotective and reluctant to release control.

### Dry humour

> “This valley was promised to our ancestors.”  
> “Your ancestors neglected to ask the river.”

## 19. Age and physique

- apparent age: 38–44;
- short-to-medium height;
- powerful legs and back from field work on unstable ground;
- compact, grounded silhouette rather than graceful goddess proportions;
- left hand missing two fingertips from lunar-glass contamination;
- movement confident on broken terrain but uncomfortable in ceremonial halls.

She should contrast with both Spas's height and Velimudr's narrow silhouette.

## 20. Face and hair

- broad face with strong jaw and high sun-weathered cheeks;
- dark hazel eyes with visible amber flecks;
- copper-brown hair heavily streaked by pale mineral dust;
- hair divided into four thick practical sections and tied behind the head;
- one dark lunar-glass burn along the left jaw and neck;
- no eternal-youth beauty, glowing goddess eyes or racial-ideal features.

## 21. Clothing

### Ground coat

A short dark-pine working coat with a broad split back and reinforced hips.
Its construction combines Rasen field cloth with repaired refugee materials.

### Pressure belt

A massive blue-grey belt carries interchangeable stone, soil and water samples.
It is the visual centre of her body and makes her silhouette low and grounded.

### Water mantle

One dark-turquoise half-mantle falls from the left shoulder and can be spread on
the ground as a moisture-measure surface. It is visibly patched with a Naga
exchange strip.

### Boots and guards

Wide-soled field boots and pale ceramic shin guards protect against unstable
ground and lunar fragments. No skirt train or barefoot nature-goddess image.

## 22. Equipment

### Returned-Ground Frame

Tara carries a folding square frame divided into four unequal test beds:

- living soil;
- salt or flood residue;
- pressure stone;
- seed and fungal recovery.

The frame opens directly onto damaged ground. It is her defining visual object.

### Lelya glass knife

A blunt-edged black lunar-glass tool used to separate contaminated material
layers. It is not a combat knife and is carried horizontally in a ceramic case.

### Covenant vessels

Three small vessels hang from the pressure belt:

- surface water;
- Lower Passage water;
- mixed settlement water.

They remain visibly separate until a local agreement authorises exchange.

### Seed debts

Small sealed packets from different refugee communities are attached to the
right hip. Each packet retains its owner colour rather than becoming one sacred
seed collection.

### Solar Covenant conductor

A short copper conductor sits beside the pressure belt. It has a dark ceramic
handle, lower grounding contact, forked upper guide and a separate exchange
strip connected to the water half-mantle. Tara uses it for her approved Perun's
Arrow ordinary attack. It is a recovered engineering instrument later described
as divine weaponry, not a spear or staff.

## 23. Palette

- dominant: dark pine green;
- structural: blue-grey stone and pale ceramic;
- water field: deep turquoise;
- warm living accent: bark red and copper brown;
- catastrophe trace: black lunar glass with restrained violet edge;
- seed-owner accents: several small distinct colours;
- no white dress, gold halo or saturated magical green.

## 24. Gameplay-object silhouette

In exact top-down cardboard representation, Tara is recognised by:

1. compact broad body and oversized pressure belt;
2. square four-bed Returned-Ground Frame carried on the right;
3. dark-turquoise half-mantle extending left;
4. three separate covenant vessels along the belt;
5. wide pale ceramic shin guards;
6. small owner-coloured seed packets at the right hip.

The short Solar Covenant conductor remains a secondary belt detail during idle
and becomes readable only during the attack. It must not replace the square
Frame as Tara's defining silhouette.

Her square and divided-ground language contrasts with Spas's circles and
Velimudr's documentary triangle.

## 25. Static pose for first concept

Tara kneels at the boundary between a flooded ruin and newly habitable ground.
The Returned-Ground Frame lies open across both surfaces.

One hand holds back officials carrying a settlement marker. The other pours a
small amount from the mixed covenant vessel into the test bed.

Behind her, a monumental stone gateway already bears the later words “Land of
Tara”, although she has not authorised the name.

## 26. Explicit exclusions

- no generic fertility goddess, dryad, druid or forest archer;
- no flower crown, antlers, wings, halo or glowing vegetation;
- no literal visual declaration that she is Perun's daughter;
- no claim that Tarkh plus Tara proves the name Tartaria;
- no white-race idealisation or blood-purity symbolism;
- no bow, sword, magical staff or combat knife;
- no pristine untouched nature aesthetic;
- no Daariyan stone mantle, lunar orrery or Velimudr text table;
- no ability effects or animation frames at this stage.

# Part III — Trio comparison

## 27. Distinct identities

| Hero | Historical problem | Character flaw | Shape language | Dominant material |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spas | recognising unavoidable catastrophe | right forecast becomes authority over others | three unequal circles | monumental pale stone and copper |
| Velimudr | words detached from speaker and edition | opposes authority while enjoying influence | imperfect triangle and unfinished board | cloth, paper, wood and clips |
| Tara | restored land converted into ancestry and empire | stable system becomes control over communities | divided square and vessels | soil, water, ceramic and lunar glass |

The three heroes do not represent warrior, mage and hunter. They represent
forecast, transmission and restoration.

## 28. Approval result — Velimudr

Approved:

- Velimudr is a personal name assigned by later editions;
- apparent age is 66–72;
- he rejects his cult while enjoying its influence;
- the folding Table of Three Versions is his defining object;
- repaired layered Belovodye clothing remains his visual foundation.

## 29. Approval result — Tara

Approved:

- Tara is the title of the first Keeper of Returned Ground;
- biological sisterhood with Dazhbog is a later political legend;
- apparent age is 38–44;
- her central conflict is restoration becoming control over communities;
- the four-bed Returned-Ground Frame is her defining object;
- she remains connected to the pseudo-historical source myth without becoming
  a literal nature goddess.
