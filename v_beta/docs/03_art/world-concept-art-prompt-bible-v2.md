# Ether Frontier — Gameplay Concept Art Prompt Bible v2

> **MATERIAL/COMPOSITION REFERENCE ONLY (2026-07-26).** Object mechanisms and
> abilities in this file are superseded by the lore-first v7 prompt framework.

Status: **canonical pre-atlas visual hypothesis prompts**  
Updated: 2026-07-24

## Goal

Test how the new lore, actual TD mechanics and cardboard art direction coexist
on a real gameplay screen. These are game-view concepts, not splash art and not
sprite atlases.

## Shared lock

- portrait top-down tower-defence board matching the real game proportions;
- two readable lanes and central defended Knot;
- exact tabletop cardboard/paper construction;
- towers larger and more saturated than environment;
- enemies readable by allegiance and combat class;
- no UI unless a dedicated UI concept call;
- no facade structures or cinematic horizon;
- no copied sacred symbols.

## C01 — Core gameplay compatibility

Use the environment prompt from `asset-prompt-bible-v6.md`. Show one early
wave, seven visibly different tower mechanisms, forest/stone economy and the
central Knot. The test question is whether route, build cells, towers and enemy
classes read within one second.

## C02 — Economy and regrowth

> Same board and camera. Focus on a Carpenter Circle cutting one resource cell,
> a Stone Circle lifting another, adjacent cells opening for construction and
> several stages of paper forest/stone regrowth elsewhere. Show temporary
> worksites as obligations to the living ground, not industrial devastation.
> Separate toothed saw, pulley, logs, basket, roots, dust and growth layers.

## C03 — Three enemy grammars

> One lane carries warm-clay Lower-Serpent retainers with individual obligation
> plates; the other carries pale Grey correction carriers and captured craft
> machines; torn translucent Nav witnesses cross between them. All are
> cardboard units with separate limbs/effects, but silhouettes and materials
> make allegiance immediately readable. No racial coding.

## C04 — Grey capture

> Before/after gameplay concept on one board: a colourful modular tower with
> owner-specific mechanism and cloth; beside it, the same coherent chassis
> captured by detachable pale directive plates, erased route card and repeated
> blank attachments. Show capture as subtraction of identity, not grey colour
> grading over the whole scene.

## C05 — Hero manifestation

> Late wave at the Last Knot. One summoned hero stands between towers and base,
> assembled from separate directional cardboard layers. Communal Answer ring is
> visibly spent into shield, slow or path effects. The hero supports the TD
> composition and does not dominate it like an action-game protagonist.

## C06 — Boss scale

> World Serpent encounter from exact overhead. A huge segmented cardboard
> serpent crosses both lanes while remaining a world force rather than a racial
> villain. Towers retain readable target functions. Reduce lesser-enemy clutter.

## C07 — UI material hypothesis

> A gameplay HUD made from flat printed ledger slips, removable resource tabs
> and compact technical diagrams. Show Zlato, Living Timber, Memory Stone,
> Bearing Names, wave preview, speed and one hero Answer ring. Blend
> constructivist information hierarchy, mid-century scientific illustration
> clarity and handmade corrections without propaganda symbols or faux runes.

## Approval gates

Each concept must be reviewed for:

1. route readability;
2. cardboard material;
3. complete separation of pieces;
4. top-down projection;
5. unique tower mechanisms;
6. enemy grammar separation;
7. cultural-source restraint;
8. compatibility with actual runtime scale;
9. absence of decorative lore noise;
10. whether it should influence sprite prompts.
