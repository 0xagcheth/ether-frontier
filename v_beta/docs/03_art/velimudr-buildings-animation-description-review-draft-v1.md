# Ether Frontier — Velimudr Buildings Animation Description Review Draft v1

Status: **rejected 2026-07-27; superseded by combat revision v2**  
Updated: 2026-07-27

## 1. Scope

This document describes animation only for Velimudr's three approved buildings:

1. Table of Three Versions;
2. Workshop of Missing Context;
3. Unfinished Board.

No art, prompts, frame counts, atlas layout, exact timings or numeric balance are
approved here. Tara's buildings are outside this review step.

## 2. Shared animation language

Velimudr's buildings do not cast words as spells. They alter gameplay through
physical comparison, attribution, revision and preservation of incomplete
records.

- exact top-down orthographic 90-degree gameplay projection;
- low monumental furniture translated into layered matte cardboard;
- source, public edition and correction always remain separate physical layers;
- every strip, clip, token and board retains a visible owner or storage place;
- no floating books, glowing runes, magical ink, paper tornado or generic aura;
- a correct result is quieter than a confident but unsupported claim;
- unknown and unresolved states must remain visually complete states.

# Part I — Table of Three Versions

## 3. Animation identity

The Table communicates **three legitimate readings with three different tactical
costs**.

Its moving parts are:

- damaged source side;
- confident public-edition side;
- detachable correction side;
- three unequal hinges;
- separate mode marker;
- one retained-reading clip unlocked at level 3.

The imperfect triangle never closes into perfect symmetry.

## 4. `place` and `idle`

### Place

1. Three supports enter the cell separately.
2. The source side is attached first and remains slightly worn and incomplete.
3. The public edition seats quickly and pulls one hinge too tight.
4. The correction side is fitted last with a visible removable clip.
5. The three supports settle at unequal distances from the centre.

### Idle

One loose source corner lifts and settles. The correction clip responds a moment
later. The public edition remains rigid, creating a readable contrast without
constant motion.

## 5. Mode selection between waves

The player rotates one physical mode marker to a side of the table. The other
two sides remain present and readable.

### Source mode

1. The damaged source opens widest.
2. A small owner plate and date attachment unfold beside it.
3. Nearby-building links extend slowly but reach farther.
4. Each linked building turns one observation layer outward.

The state must feel cautious and broad, not weak or broken.

### Public Edition mode

1. The confident edition slides above the other two sides by one cardboard layer.
2. Its repeated action strips align into one clean stack.
3. Links to nearby buildings shorten and become mechanically direct.
4. Their preparation parts begin moving sooner and repeat more regularly.

A duplicate join remains visible on every strip, telegraphing the increased risk
of Grey copying.

### Correction mode

1. The detachable correction side opens across the public edition but does not
   cover the damaged source.
2. One revision clip remains raised and ready.
3. Nearby links end in open brackets rather than closed action strips.
4. On the first interaction with an imposed enemy layer, the bracket closes,
   pulls that layer out of alignment and then returns to the Table.

## 6. Locked wave state and resolution

At wave start, unequal hinges receive three separate locking wedges. The mode
marker cannot rotate until the wave ends.

At resolution:

- Source returns all observation pieces to their owners;
- Public Edition removes duplicated strips one by one;
- Correction retrieves the used bracket and stores it apart from unused clips.

There is no universal success flourish.

## 7. Level 3 retained reading

One selected nearby building may retain the previous mode:

1. a small copy of the previous mode marker detaches from the Table;
2. it travels along the existing physical link;
3. it locks to that building's owner layer;
4. the main Table changes mode normally;
5. after the wave, the retained marker returns before another can be assigned.

This is retained context, not two simultaneous global modes.

## 8. `damage` and `destroy`

Damage loosens one hinge and offsets the public-edition stack. Destruction opens
all three sides flat around the supports. The correction side detaches cleanly;
the damaged source remains attached to its first support; no records burn or
vanish.

# Part II — Workshop of Missing Context

## 9. Animation identity

The Workshop communicates **one building receiving a maintained, revocable
footnote**.

Its moving parts are:

- open central work surface;
- owner strip, action strip, target-relation strip and revocation strip;
- oversized copper revision clips;
- unfinished wooden board;
- one transfer rail toward the protected building;
- a second strip rack only at level 3.

## 10. `place` and empty `idle`

1. A rectangular stone-and-wood frame seats into the cell.
2. The central surface unfolds but deliberately leaves one corner open.
3. Four different source strips enter separate racks.
4. Copper clips are placed jaw-up beside their corresponding strips.
5. The unfinished board closes the rear edge without filling the open corner.

In idle, a single clip tests an empty strip end and releases it. The Workshop
must look ready for attribution, not like a production conveyor.

## 11. Attaching `Restore the Footnote`

1. The selected building sends its owner plate to the Workshop temporarily.
2. The Workshop places it at the centre of the work surface.
3. Four strips are compared against it in fixed order: owner, action, legitimate
   target relationship, revocation condition.
4. One distinct clip joins the four strips without fusing them.
5. The completed context strip travels along the transfer rail.
6. It attaches beneath the protected building's owner layer, leaving all four
   ends visible.
7. The owner plate returns to the protected building.

The Workshop then becomes `maintaining`; it cannot create another strip.

## 12. Protection responses

### First owner-removal attempt

The owner end of the context strip catches the imposed Grey layer. The copper
clip rotates, holds the original owner plate in place and leaves the attacking
attachment exposed. Only this first attempt is refused.

### Copied or disguised enemy layer

The action and target-relation ends separate. Their mismatch opens a visible gap
in the disguise, allowing the protected building to recognise the relevant
layer.

### Interruption recovery

The revocation end releases the protected building's jammed active part and
returns it to neutral through a short physical pull. It does not undo damage.

### Duplication attempt

All four ends tighten around the owner plate. The Engine may copy the building's
shape, but the maintained relationship does not transfer.

## 13. Moving the context strip

1. The protected building stops receiving benefits.
2. The four-ended strip returns intact to the Workshop.
3. Every clip is removed and stored separately.
4. The first building's owner plate is returned.
5. Only then may the Workshop begin attribution for a new building.

The transfer creates a deliberate unprotected interval for both targets.

## 14. Level 3, damage and destruction

Level 3 adds a second strip rack on the opposite side. The two completed strips
must use visibly different action-family attachments.

Damage bends one transfer rail and dislodges an unused clip. Destruction folds
the work surface inward. Active context strips detach from the Workshop but stay
with their protected owners only long enough to complete a safe withdrawal;
they do not become permanent free bonuses.

# Part III — Unfinished Board

## 15. Animation identity

The Board communicates **the first occurrence is preserved; later identical
claims become recognisable; the missing ending stays missing**.

Its moving parts are:

- monumental horizontal board with absent final section;
- three source holders;
- speaker-present token;
- one to three record slots by level;
- sequence pegs;
- target and provenance attachments;
- comparison frame around, never across, the empty section.

## 16. `place` and unrecorded `idle`

1. The stone-and-metal comparison frame is assembled first.
2. The wooden board enters in two surviving sections.
3. The absent final section remains an open part of the silhouette.
4. Three source holders close around different edges without touching the gap.
5. The speaker-present token rests outside the frame.

During idle, one empty sequence peg tests a record slot and returns. Nothing
attempts to fill the absent section.

## 17. Recording the first completed special action

The first occurrence succeeds normally.

1. A provenance piece arrives from the hostile action's source.
2. Separate sequence pegs arrive in the actual order of preparation and effect.
3. A target attachment arrives from the affected owner.
4. The speaker-present token remains outside unless a real present witness
   supplied evidence.
5. The Board assembles these pieces in one record slot.
6. It leaves an open end after the last known step.

The Board performs no prevention animation during this first occurrence.

## 18. Comparing a later action

1. A new sequence approaches an occupied record slot.
2. Each piece is compared physically: source, order, target and imposed layer.
3. Matching pieces align; different pieces remain offset and open a new record.
4. Only a fully identical sequence closes the comparison frame around its slot.
5. A warning path extends toward nearby appropriate buildings.
6. The repeated hostile preparation visibly slows while those buildings prepare.

The animation distinguishes similarity from identity rather than treating every
enemy action as the same category.

## 19. Third identical repetition at level 3

1. Two prior sequence notches are already visible beside the record.
2. The third identical sequence closes all source and target attachments.
3. The comparison frame extends around the recorded section but stops before the
   missing final section.
4. An appropriate nearby building receives the exposed commitment point.
5. The hostile action is interrupted by that building, not by a blast from the
   Board itself.
6. A third notch is added; the record remains revisable.

## 20. Unknown and different actions

An unrecognised action is not forced into an existing slot. Its pieces remain on
an open holder with an `unknown` spacing pattern. A different known action opens
a new slot if capacity permits. At full capacity it remains visible outside the
frame rather than disappearing or being falsely classified.

## 21. `upgrade`, `damage`, `destroy`

- level 1 exposes one record slot;
- level 2 unfolds a second holder from beneath the left surviving board section;
- level 3 adds a third holder and the comparison arms required for interruption.

Damage cracks a source holder or bends a sequence peg while preserving existing
records. On destruction, the frame opens outward, record assemblies settle
beside their slots and the two surviving board sections fall apart without ever
closing the absent final section.

## 22. Separation test

- Table of Three Versions: imperfect triangle choosing one of three side states;
- Workshop of Missing Context: rectangle sending one maintained four-ended strip;
- Unfinished Board: large broken rectangle accumulating comparable sequences.

No activation may be mistaken for a book spell or generic support aura.
