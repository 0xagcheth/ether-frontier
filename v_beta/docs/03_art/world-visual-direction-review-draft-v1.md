# Ether Frontier — World Visual Direction v1

Status: **canonical world visual direction; approved 2026-07-26**  
Updated: 2026-07-26

## Approved scope

This document defines only the overall external image of the world. It does not
approve heroes, buildings, enemies, abilities, animations, prompts or assets.

Approved decisions:

- the world is primarily stone-built and monumental;
- cosmic technology is present but subordinate to mass, age and architecture;
- the cardboard tabletop material applies to gameplay objects, not to the
  world's narrative reality;
- Daariya, Asgard Iriysky and Antlan are the three primary visual anchors.

## Core image

Ether Frontier is a ruined Slavic-Aryan cosmic civilisation as its believers
would imagine it: impossibly ancient, technologically powerful and filled with
physical evidence of gods, several moons, interworld ships and forgotten wars.

The satirical layer is not expressed through jokes painted onto the scenery.
It appears through contradictions inside the environment: two monuments date
the same event differently; a sacred temple contains a maintenance hatch; a
“primordial” wall has a later repair beneath its oldest layer; four maps of one
city disagree while each still operates part of it.

The world must initially inspire wonder. Only on closer inspection does it
become clear that the official explanation has been assembled from different
eras and owners.

## Overall visual formula

**Ancient monumental stone civilisation + lost polar homeland + restrained
cosmic engineering + traces of lunar and nuclear catastrophes.**

The world is not medieval Rus with spaceships. It is a separate civilisation
whose enormous stone architecture developed around orbital mechanics, water
pressure, ancestral records and interworld routes. Technology is embedded in
walls, plazas, channels and monoliths rather than dominating them as exposed
science-fiction machinery.

## Shapes

- immense circular and crescent stone structures derived from moon orbits;
- radial settlements divided by four waterways or processional routes;
- broad low monumental buildings designed to be read from above;
- layered landing courts for Vaitmanas rather than towers with roofs;
- enormous stone route rings with restrained metal joints embedded into the
  landscape;
- asymmetry produced by repairs after repeated catastrophes;
- broken orbital diagrams continuing directly into streets and canals.

Vertical onion domes, Viking halls, generic wooden villages and golden fantasy
palaces are excluded.

## Materials

Inside the fiction:

- pale polar stone holding pressure marks;
- dark timber grown into structural curves;
- copper and bronze orbital conductors;
- black meteor material from destroyed moons;
- translucent lunar glass;
- white ceramic plates surviving extreme heat;
- woven route cloth used to encode direction;
- water channels treated as part of architecture, not decoration.

In narrative art these materials are depicted as real stone, timber, ceramic,
metal, water and lunar glass. Cardboard construction is reserved for gameplay
objects, sprites and the playable board representation.

## Colour

The base world is not brown medieval fantasy.

- polar stone: cold blue-grey and chalk white;
- living structures: pine green, bark red and birch cream;
- orbital mechanisms: oxidised copper, muted gold and deep indigo;
- water systems: dark turquoise and silver;
- lunar debris: charcoal black with pale violet fracture edges;
- active ancient technology: warm white and amber, never neon;
- Grey interference: ash-lilac overlays that remove local colour.

## The sky

The ancient sky is defined by three visibly different moons:

- Lelya — small, rapid, inhabited and mechanically dense;
- Fatta — large, scarred, visibly imported and unevenly repaired;
- Mesyats — calm, distant and used as the stable reference.

Vaitmanas and Vaitmaras are not flying saucers. They resemble travelling cities
assembled from nested route rings, pressure ribs, folded habitat plates and
replaceable repair sections.

## Daariya

Daariya is the visual ideal of the lost world: a polar circular continent cut
into four territories by engineered rivers. At its centre is the Northern
Well, where orbital routes meet the planet.

From a distance it looks perfectly ordered. Closer inspection reveals that the
four territories use different construction systems and that the “sacred”
rivers follow older Naga pressure channels.

Key impression: magnificent, cold, inhabited, administratively overconfident.

## Asgard Iriysky

Asgard is not a palace-city. It is a huge exchange junction built where the
Iriy and Om waterways meet. Its major landmarks are landing courts, date
wheels, treaty halls, market bridges and lunar-control platforms.

Different eras have attached incompatible wings to the same civic structures.
Every district insists it is the original centre.

Key impression: prosperous cosmic capital that still feels like a working
transport city.

## Antlan

Antlan consists of seven inhabited island rings connected by tide locks,
copper memory channels and floating courts. Its technology is smoother and
more aquatic than Daariya's, but visibly built from refugee knowledge.

The city should look genuinely desirable before its authoritarian emergency
systems become visible. The same gates that distribute water can isolate and
drown a district.

Key impression: beautiful civic machine slowly becoming a control system.

## Ruined world

Present-day ruins contain evidence of several different catastrophes:

- lunar fragments embedded in polar stone;
- drowned Antlan channels far inland;
- fused ground from foundry charges;
- buried lower floors caused by local rebuilding and flooding;
- collapsed pressure routes mistaken for gigantic bones;
- later monuments installed directly over older machines.

They must never look as if one identical explosion destroyed everything.

## Gameplay-object presentation

Gameplay objects translate the monumental world into premium physical
punchboard pieces. The cardboard style is not a property of the fictional
world and not children's craft. It is the player's tabletop representation:

- each historical era becomes a separate printed material layer;
- repairs visibly overlap earlier construction;
- removable labels show changing ownership;
- orbital bodies are suspended card mechanisms;
- water is made from layered translucent and metallic paper;
- destruction separates layers rather than creating realistic fireballs.

## Proposed emotional sequence

1. **Wonder:** impossible ancient cosmic scale.
2. **Recognition:** familiar mythic names and shapes.
3. **Suspicion:** dates, maps and materials contradict each other.
4. **Material truth:** the ruins prove that something happened, but not the
   official total explanation.
5. **Satirical clarity:** the grand myth survives by relabelling every
   contradiction as enemy sabotage.

## Approval result

The world direction is approved with monumental stone as the dominant visual
language. Cardboard remains mandatory only for gameplay representation.
Daariya, Asgard Iriysky and Antlan are approved as the main visual centres.
