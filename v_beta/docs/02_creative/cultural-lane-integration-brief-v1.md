# Ether Frontier — Cultural Lane Integration Brief v1

Status: **pre-canon design brief**  
Updated: 2026-07-24

## 1. New creative thesis

Ether Frontier is not about proving that one lost civilisation invented every
people. It is about defending a world whose survival depends on different
communities preserving different names, obligations and methods of memory.

The Greys do not conquer land in the ordinary sense. They make every account
equally blank, every tower interchangeable and every loss reversible only as an
empty copy. Their ideal victory is a perfectly efficient world in which nothing
has a name worth defending.

The player protects **the Knot of Seven Roads**, where seven traditions of
memory meet without becoming one tradition. This is the tower-defence reason
for the conflict: the Knot keeps the Mother Earth capable of distinguishing
soil from scar, ancestor from duplicate, lawful ending from deletion, and
promise from command.

## 2. Cosmology that can support the game

### The Living Surface

The visible battlefield is the Mother Earth's skin. Build cells are not empty
slots: each has moisture, strain, buried memory and a tolerance for extraction.
A defence can protect the road while still wounding the ground.

### The Buried Memory

Below the surface are unfinished obligations, repaired cities, serpent
homelands, drowned Antlan channels and sealed Grey strata. They are not one
undifferentiated underworld.

### Mara's Limit

Mara is not the final villain. She is the right of a form to end. Without her,
dead units repeat, ruins cannot become soil and grief becomes a hostile loop.
Grey erasure is her opposite: it removes the name as if the life never
happened.

### Iriy

Iriy is not a reward screen or a generic heaven. It is the preserved route by
which a name may be recalled correctly. A name reaches Iriy only when another
being remembers the obligations attached to it.

### The Rule

Prav/Rule is a disputed human term for the constraints connecting worlds. In
the game it is not divine bureaucracy. It is the set of contracts that no
faction can alter alone.

### The Grey Interval

When names, distinctions and witnesses are stripped away, a non-place opens
between layers. Greys enter through this interval. Their bodies are temporary
templates assembled from erased local forms.

## 3. Seven roads, not seven ethnic factions

`Seven Roads` is an original Ether Frontier coalition and a continuation of
the selected Option C structure. It is not presented as a historically
attested sacred system shared by the source cultures. The number and grouping
remain provisional until the campaign-relevant lanes are approved.

Each Road is a fictional coalition with a focused source lane. No Road claims
to represent a real people in full.

### Road I — The Soil Oath

Source emphasis: East Slavic agrarian and lower-mythology material.

Gameplay:

- heals or exhausts build cells;
- records every structure removed from the ground;
- gains strength from leaving selected cells unbuilt;
- negotiates with weather, household and landscape agents.

Visual construction:

- timber laminations, linen hinges, birch-bark records, forged clamps;
- moist dark soil and root channels;
- symbols are invented contract marks, not copied ritual signs.

Narrative tension:

- its archivists want one clean ancient canon;
- its gardeners know that local practice has always been contradictory.

### Road II — The Mountain Water

Source emphasis: Bashkir epic principles, with a separate Tatar/Volga-Bulgar
historical lane where used.

Gameplay:

- redirects water rather than simply generating currency;
- opens a winged movement path after a communal sacrifice;
- resolves bosses by finishing the correct sequence of an epic obligation.

Visual construction:

- layered felt-card, leather hinges, mountain-stone rings, wood and metal;
- sweeping movement silhouettes derived from function, not copied tamgas.

Narrative tension:

- a hero can keep the water of life personally or release it to the land;
- the player's tower build must embody that choice.

### Road III — The Returning Voice

Source emphasis: distinct Central Asian epic-performance and craft lanes,
including Uzbek and Karakalpak bakhshi art, Kyrgyz manaschi practice and
Alpamysh/Alpomish variant traditions.

Gameplay:

- support effects are built from call, instrument, refrain and audience answer;
- portable modules fold and move between cells;
- a broken road is restored by a correct promise sequence, not by raw damage.

Visual construction:

- warp-dyed paper bands, felt, carved wood, ceramic and metal accents;
- deliberate print blur and registration drift;
- each specific community source receives its own prompt and review record.

Narrative tension:

- written archives preserve wording but can kill variation;
- oral performance preserves variation but can lose a route when its bearers
  are separated.

### Road IV — The Seven Depths

Source emphasis: a specifically Mari-inspired cosmogonic lane, later expanded
through separate Udmurt, Komi, Erzya, Moksha, Karelian and other research.

Gameplay:

- a diving-bird mechanism retrieves submerged memory tokens;
- maps exist in seven depth states;
- every created terrain feature produces a counter-obligation.

Visual construction:

- water rings, branching wood-card, birch bark, textile rhythm and small metal
  pendants transformed beyond recognisable sacred use.

Narrative tension:

- the road's paired creators disagree over whether imperfection is sabotage or
  the condition of a living world.

### Road V — The Long Named Song

Source emphasis: Sakha/Yakut Olonkho and carefully separated northern,
Siberian and Far-Eastern storytelling/material lanes.

Gameplay:

- a three-register performed sequence changes which world a unit occupies;
- seasonal state changes open and close routes;
- characters with long names resist Grey template replacement.

Visual construction:

- wood, hide, birch bark, bone-like card, frost fibre and repaired field
  infrastructure;
- no generic sacred drum, mask or “shaman class”.

Narrative tension:

- shortening a name makes commands faster but severs remembered relations;
- the player chooses between tactical speed and ontological resilience.

### Road VI — The Stone Chorus

Source emphasis: distinct Nart-cycle lanes, Armenian epic performance,
Georgian polyphonic principles and Azerbaijani/Turkic storyteller traditions,
never blended into a single ethnic style.

Gameplay:

- mountain gates interlock physically;
- several weak voices create a field that no solo unit can produce;
- guest-right, oath and household memory change mission rules.

Visual construction:

- interlocking stone-card, metal, wood, vine and layered acoustic rings;
- specific architecture is used only after lane-specific research.

Narrative tension:

- communities share some stories but disagree on heroes, kinship and moral;
- the Knot survives only if the disagreements remain visible.

### Road VII — The Woven Counterpoint

Source emphasis: Baltic multipart-song and material traditions, explicitly
non-Slavic.

Gameplay:

- staggered pulses overlap into defensive zones;
- no single pulse is the melody or the shield;
- removing apparent “duplication” destroys the combined effect.

Visual construction:

- flax, straw-card, wood, amber-coloured resin substitute and restrained woven
  rhythm;
- no copied crosses or sacred/community emblems.

Narrative tension:

- Grey optimisation identifies the voices as redundant;
- the player demonstrates that their offset is the actual function.

## 4. How Daariya, Antlan, Greys and Serpents fit

### Daariya

`Daariya` is a provisional and potentially unsuitable name because it carries
strong associations with the racialised Slavic-Aryan alternative-history
corpus. If retained at all, it should be the disputed name found in one
compromised in-world source, not the neutral narrator's accepted terminology.

Daariya is a lost polar engineering network, not the biological ancestor of
modern peoples. Its builders attempted to connect the Seven Roads through
celestial route machinery. Every surviving archive describes them differently.
The uncertainty is permanent and intentional.

Function:

- explains old orbital mechanisms and the broken Moon Cards;
- supplies ruins, not a superior playable race;
- creates the temptation of a single-origin theory.

### Antlan

Antlan was a hydraulic archive civilisation. It tried to make memory independent
of witnesses by storing every obligation in water pressure, copper channels and
repeatable machines. The system survived its people but gradually confused
recording with truth.

Function:

- provides drowned maps and water-routing technology;
- mirrors the Grey temptation without making Antlan evil;
- introduces the question: can a perfect record remain alive?

### Lower-Serpent peoples

Several peoples live below the surface and disagree among themselves. Their
bodies and architecture are adapted to heat, stone pressure, moulting and
underground water. They are not “reptilian elites”.

Functions:

- allies, traders, rivals and enemies can all occur;
- shed skins provide a natural metaphor for identity without loss of memory;
- their routes distinguish Nav from an inhabited lower world.

### Greys

Greys have no original culture accessible to the player. They appear wherever a
system replaces relationships with identical templates. They can possess a
machine, a false archive, a copied soldier or a defence tower.

Functions:

- justify waves and tower capture;
- turn lore falsification into battlefield mechanics;
- create silhouettes through subtraction, repetition and missing labels.

## 5. Proposed player cast

### The Binder

Player-role character. Does not command the Seven Roads as an emperor. The
Binder negotiates temporary assemblies and is accountable for ground strain,
broken promises and false archive links.

### Veda, the Doubting Archivist

Working name requiring replacement or explicit approval. Keeps the
witness/evidence/use registry. She begins by believing
that one authentic master text will restore the world. Her arc is learning that
provenance and disagreement are stronger than purity.

### Mera, Keeper of the Living Measure

Working name requiring replacement because it is too easily confused with
Mara. Embodies the Mother Earth's readable state without literally
personifying the whole Earth as a human queen. She senses moisture, scar,
buried pressure and whether a build is a contract or an extraction.

### Mara, Keeper of the Last Door

Neither ally nor enemy by default. She ends Nav loops, decomposes destroyed
forms into memory and refuses resurrection without a witness. Her conflict with
the player concerns when defence becomes refusal to let anything end.

### The Long-Name Singer

An Olonkho-inspired fictional performer whose full name changes as obligations
are accepted. Greys repeatedly try to shorten the name into a unit ID.

### The Returning Bakhshi

A fictional storyteller-engineer from the Returning Voice Road. Repairs
portable defences by restoring the missing relation between verses, audience
and route. No exact real epic text is copied.

### The Diver

A Seven Depths route-reader operating a bird-shaped retrieval machine. They
recover memories from flooded layers but must return an equivalent gift to the
water.

### The Serpent Cartographer

An envoy from one Lower-Serpent community. Demonstrates early that the lower
world, Nav and Grey Interval are different places. Their maps are read through
heat and pressure, not surface light.

### The Grey Editor

Primary antagonist voice. It never argues for destruction. It offers clarity:
one origin, one corrected name, one efficient tower, one harmless version of
the past. Each “correction” removes a relationship needed for the world to
function.

## 6. Tower-defence conflict

The Knot of Seven Roads is also a stabilising machine. During each wave, the
Grey Editor sends copied forms along a road whose name has been weakened.
Enemies do not seek a conventional castle. They seek the **Bearing Ledger** at
the centre, which tells the Mother Earth which route belongs to which promise.

Why fixed paths exist:

- roads are obligations made physically traversable;
- Greys can enter only along a route that has lost part of its name;
- defenders cannot simply close every route because communities, water,
  seasons and the dead also require passage.

Why towers exist:

- towers are assembled witnesses;
- each tower combines a stable local base, a lane-specific functional module
  and replaceable animated child layers;
- captured towers lose unique parts first and become generic Grey templates.

Why waves escalate:

- every successful defence reveals more of the Ledger;
- every careless extraction injures the Earth and opens a lower shortcut;
- every convenient false interpretation gives the Grey Editor a future entry
  point.

What victory means:

- not exterminating an enemy species;
- restoring enough named relations that the Grey Interval cannot remain open;
- accepting Mara's closure of the obsolete central machine;
- leaving the Seven Roads connected but irreducibly different.

## 7. Gameplay systems derived from the research

### Witness / Evidence / Use

Codex discoveries have three parallel values. Players may act on uncertain
material, but the interface never converts usefulness into proof.

### Ground strain

Every build adds pressure, extraction and moisture change. Repairing the ground
can be a stronger long-term action than upgrading a weapon.

### Performed sequences

Some towers require ordered layers—voice, instrument, answer, movement—rather
than a single cooldown. Silence, interruption and wrong order are meaningful
enemy attacks.

### Named modularity

Shared tower bases are allowed, but the unique functional module must express
the object's actual lore and mechanic. No universal crossbow family.

### Lawful ending

Mara abilities end loops, retire obsolete towers, close paths and transform
debris into remembered components. This is mechanically distinct from selling,
deleting or Grey conversion.

### False kinship

The player may force two unrelated fragments to “match”. This grants an
immediate build discount or damage bonus, then increases the probability of
misidentification, friendly targeting or tower capture.

## 8. Soviet and post-Soviet material layer

This layer concerns lived material history, not a replacement mythology.

Use:

- paper manuals, technical diagrams and archive cards;
- constructivist clarity for composition, not ideological slogans;
- public-mosaic colour logic translated into cut paper;
- enamel signs, repair dates, reused fasteners and locally adapted machinery;
- cosmic optimism in tension with abandoned infrastructure;
- inexpensive board-game printing, misregistration and hand correction;
- differences between regions remain visible beneath shared industrial parts.

Avoid:

- direct state emblems and propaganda slogans;
- making every culture visually Russian;
- “post-Soviet” as a synonym for dirt, rust or collapse;
- anonymous exotic ornament pasted onto standard machinery.

## 9. Art-direction consequences

The approved texture direction remains:

- true top-down orthographic camera;
- cardboard, paper, printed fibre, shallow stacking and visible cut edges;
- attractive stylisation with slightly exaggerated functional silhouettes;
- no glossy 3D render and no facade view.

New requirements:

- every culturally meaningful element must be a separately readable card,
  fibre, hinge, plate or printed insert;
- depth comes from many shallow layers and cast-paper contact shadows;
- unique gameplay function gets the strongest size, colour and silhouette
  accent;
- no decoration is added without a material, narrative or mechanical reason;
- concept art may show assembled scenes; sprite sources must later decompose all
  moving and swappable parts.
