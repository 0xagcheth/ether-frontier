# Ether Frontier — Game Lore Bible v6

Status: **canonical lore for runtime migration**  
Canon title: **Семь кругов Ирия**  
Campaign subtitle: **Семь дорог последнего узла**  
Updated: 2026-07-24

## 1. High concept

The world survives because different communities remember it differently.
Their songs, measurements, graves, repairs, names and routes overlap without
becoming one perfect story.

At the Last Knot, two visible Roads and seven traditions of keeping memory meet
above older layers: drowned hydraulic records, serpent homelands, sealed cities
and a polar route-engine known only through contradictory fragments.

The Greys enter wherever a living relation is replaced by one efficient blank
template. They do not want territory. They want the **Bearing Ledger**, the
device that lets the Mother Earth distinguish road from wound, the dead from a
duplicate, and a promise from an order.

The player is the Binder. During each Grey pulse, the Binder assembles temporary
cardboard-like craft forms from Zlato, Living Timber and Memory Stone, holds the
Roads and decides which stories may remain incompatible.

## 2. Source policy inside the fiction

The game never announces one real mythology as the secret history of all
peoples.

Every discovery has:

- **witness** — who tells it;
- **evidence** — what exists materially;
- **reading** — how the witness connects the evidence;
- **use** — what action the reading enables;
- **risk** — what disappears if the reading becomes mandatory.

The disputed Veles Fragments, Daariya claims, Antlan records, several Moon
Cards and buried-city theories are powerful in-world sources but never
omniscient narration.

## 3. The world layers

Different Roads use different names. The following are Binder terminology, not
a universal ancient theology.

### Yav — the Made Surface

Material action, bodies, roads, weather and craft. Greys need carriers to act
here and can therefore be physically opposed.

### The Mother Earth

The living substrate of the board. She is not represented as one human queen.
Moisture, roots, pressure, scars and regrowth are her readable condition.

### Nav — Unfinished Memory

Traces that retain an obligation but lack completion. Nav beings may be
hostile, confused or recoverable.

### Mara's Limit

An Ether Frontier synthesis: the lawful end of a form. Mara closes loops,
accepts debris and prevents resurrection without a witness. She is not the same
as Grey erasure.

### Iriy — the Remembered Return

Not a universal heaven. It is the route by which a correctly held name can
return to relation with the living.

### Inhabited Depths

Homes of multiple Lower-Serpent peoples, with water, heat, craft, law and
political disagreement. The Depths are neither Nav nor hell.

### Grey Interval

A non-place created by erased difference. Its inhabitants can repeat forms but
cannot originate a living relation.

## 4. The Last Knot

The Knot was not built by one superior civilisation. Generations added to it:

- an older orientation bed of unknown origin;
- Antlan pressure channels;
- serpent depth anchors;
- surface stone and timber works;
- seven later Road sockets;
- twentieth-century measuring, enamel and repair parts;
- contemporary hand-written corrections.

It cannot be moved because it is not merely a machine. Its bearings include
the local watershed, graves, root systems, subterranean pressure and promises
made at that exact crossing.

## 5. Seven Roads

Seven is the coalition's practical assembly rule, not a proven shared sacred
number.

1. **Soil Oath** — land, restraint, household and living boundary.
2. **Mountain Water** — communal flow, sacrifice and return.
3. **Returning Voice** — epic performance, promise and improvisation.
4. **Seven Depths** — layered world, diving retrieval and counter-obligation.
5. **Long Named Song** — voice, climate, journey and resistant naming.
6. **Stone Chorus** — oath, guest-right and several voices holding one gate.
7. **Woven Counterpoint** — offset parts whose difference creates the shield.

Each is a fictional coalition inspired by a limited, documented source lane.
No Road represents an entire real people.

## 5.1. Seven Circles of Iriy

The Seven Roads and Seven Circles are not the same thing.

- **Roads** are coalitions of witnesses, practices and obligations.
- **Circles** are functional craft assemblies around the Last Knot.

The Circles are named Root, Stone, Thunder, Sun, Wind, Water and Name. They are
not seven gods, races, nations or universal cosmic levels. Each Circle can be
operated only when at least one Road supplies a witness, material, intended use
and accepted risk. This prevents any one cultural Road from permanently owning
one military or economic function.

The public title **Seven Circles of Iriy** refers to the seven assemblies
working together to keep a correctly witnessed return possible. The campaign
subtitle **Seven Roads of the Last Knot** refers to the coalition that makes
those assemblies legitimate.

## 6. The Andrboll arc

The campaign mirrors the escalation examined by Andrboll:

1. amusing resemblance;
2. comforting lost origin;
3. supposedly authentic master text;
4. total explanation;
5. immunity to contradiction;
6. demand for purity;
7. erasure of people who do not fit.

The Grey Editor rarely lies outright. It edits uncertainty out of true
fragments until only one compulsory origin remains.

## 7. Factions

### Keepers of the Knot

Craft workers, singers, route readers, local spirits and defenders who accept
temporary common rules without surrendering their different histories.

### Lower-Serpent communities

Not one faction. Some defend the Depths, some trade, some follow military
princes, and some are coerced by the Greys. Their visual language is clay,
pressure-fit stone, shed layers, heat-sensitive paper and underground water —
never conspiracy-coded luxury or real ethnic caricature.

### Nav witnesses

Unfinished forms seeking completion. Some attack because the Grey Editor gives
them a false missing name.

### Greys

A distributed editing protocol expressed through pale carrier bodies,
interruption tools and captured machines. The **Grey Editor** is the coherent
voice produced when enough local Grey instances share one directive.

### Mara

Independent keeper of endings. She will help close a Grey loop but oppose a
Binder who refuses to let obsolete defences, institutions or heroes end.

## 8. Main characters

### The Binder

Player role. Reads the Ledger, places temporary forms and is accountable for
ground strain and false links. Never becomes ruler of the Roads.

### Rada Sveden — keeper of provenance

Replacement for working name `Veda`. A practical archivist who records witness,
evidence and use. She begins by searching for one master text and learns to
preserve contradiction without surrendering judgement.

### Zema — reader of living ground

Replacement for working name `Mera`. A fictional non-ethnic title, not a direct
deity. Reads moisture, pressure and whether construction is a contract or
extraction.

### Mara, Keeper of the Last Door

Calm, exact and unsentimental. She does not kill for pleasure; she insists that
forms end honestly.

### Borislav the Thunder Keeper — runtime `paladin`

Frontline defender. Converts communal Answer into shields. His flaw is
believing protection justifies permanent command.

### Ayana the Name Reader — runtime `mage`

Ranged controller and interpreter of incompatible records. She slows enemies
by restoring the missing relation in their carrier instructions. Her name is
fictional and must not be presented as representative of one real people.

### Amba the Pathfinder — runtime `hunter`

Taiga route keeper, skirmisher and reader of animal paths. She earns more from
ground threats because she recovers what raiders leave behind.

### Sarma the Depth Cartographer

Serpent envoy. Proves that Depths, Nav and Grey Interval are different. Reads
maps through heat and pressure.

### The Grey Editor

Antagonist voice. Offers one origin, one corrected name and one perfectly
efficient tower. Its final proposal is peaceful: erase every distinction that
could create conflict.

## 9. Plot

### Prologue — The convenient resemblance

The Binder arrives during a small incursion. Rada demonstrates fragments whose
similar signs seem to prove one ancient source. The first victory makes that
theory operational.

### Act I — A text that explains everything

The defence restores Soil, Stone and Wind mechanisms. Military Lower-Serpent
groups attack, apparently confirming the master text's enemy list. Sarma
reveals that several raiders carry surface contracts and were driven upward by
Grey pressure.

Boss: Red-Scale Voivode. His defeat yields two incompatible versions of the
same treaty.

### Act II — The corrected sky

The Grey Editor captures craft forms and removes their unique owner layers.
Rada's master reading predicts every attack but increasingly misidentifies
allies. The second Road opens.

Bosses: Prince of the Depths, Three-Headed Sky Keeper and Empty-Name Engine.
The player learns that Antlan failed by making records independent of living
witnesses.

### Act III — Purity

The Editor offers a complete polar-origin history. It explains the Moon Cards,
Antlan, the Depths and every Road by deleting contradictory evidence. Nav
witnesses become generic enemies; serpent allies become “biological threats”;
the defence becomes more efficient and less alive.

Mara demands closure of the obsolete central orientation engine. The player
must defend the Knot while unbinding the very machine being defended.

Final encounters: Grey Conductor and Awakened World Serpent.

### Endings

1. **One restored order** — stable defence, but contradiction is forced into
   marginal notes; Grey risk remains.
2. **Many-road covenant** — the Ledger becomes distributed among witnesses;
   harder to operate, harder to capture.
3. **Mara's closure** — the central engine ends, Roads survive locally and the
   next generation must build a new connection.

No ending proves one perfect ancestral origin.

## 10. Visual canon

The whole game looks like a premium handmade board-game diorama:

- exact top-down orthographic board;
- warm printed paper and smooth solid punchboard;
- visible tan cut edges;
- each physical element has its own complete perimeter;
- small gaps and compact contact shadows create depth;
- slightly cartooned functional silhouettes;
- primary mechanisms are larger and more saturated than support pieces;
- limited printed texture tied to lore and material;
- Soviet/post-Soviet influence through dated print, engineering diagrams,
  enamel, mosaic colour blocks, repair marks and cosmic instruments;
- no glossy 3D, side facade, generic runes or sacred-symbol collage.

## 11. Canon prohibitions

- no real ethnic group as enemy species;
- no racial hierarchy as world truth;
- no universal crossbow family;
- no copied living sacred signs;
- no “all former-USSR cultures are one mythology”;
- no Daariya as neutral confirmed history;
- no Yav/Nav/Prav as the objective vocabulary of every Road;
- no realism that loses cardboard construction;
- no ornament without mechanical or material meaning.
