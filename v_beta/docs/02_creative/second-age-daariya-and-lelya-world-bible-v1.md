# Ether Frontier — Second Age: Daariya and Lelya World Bible v1

Status: **canonical second-age world layer**  
Updated: 2026-07-26

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the second age of Ether Frontier: the settlement of
Daariya, the founding of Asgard Iriysky, Midgard's three moons, the occupation
of Lelya, Spas's warning, Tarkh Dazhbog's strike and the flood that ends the
polar homeland.

It adapts the pseudo-history examined in Andrboll's documentary. Characters
inside the world may sincerely call the events sacred history, but the game
separates physical evidence, testimony, interpretation and later propaganda.

The player must first experience Daariya as an astonishing civilisation worth
saving. Satire enters through its institutions and contradictions, never by
making its inhabitants foolish caricatures.

## 2. Daariya under three moons

Daariya occupies a polar landmass around the Northern Well, a stable junction
between Midgard and the Golden Path. Four great waterways divide it into
districts associated with the four fleet lineages.

The waterways are not natural rivers. They combine meltwater channels,
pressure-relief trenches and ancient Naga routes that existed before the First
Landing. Fleet histories credit the settlers with creating them because the
settlers added names and customs stations.

Above Daariya move three moons:

| Moon | Public description | Material function |
|---|---|---|
| **Lelya** | the swift youngest moon | nearest transfer station, water regulator and passage anchor |
| **Fatta** | the adopted wandering moon | heavy orbital reservoir brought from the damaged Deya system |
| **Mesyats** | the measured moon | stable calendar and tidal reference that survives into later ages |

Their celebrated periods of seven, thirteen and twenty-nine-and-a-half days
belong to different calendars. Later teachers place the three numbers in one
table and call the arithmetic proof of an unchanged ancient science.

## 3. The four districts

### Da'Aryan Reach

The northern approach and orbital docks. Its people keep Vaitmaras in slow
circuits and preserve routes that may disappear for generations.

Its ruling Registry of Arrival gradually treats being listed first as evidence
of owning the whole continent.

### Kh'Aryan Measure

The observatories, dimensional weather towers and schools of layered writing.
It maintains several valid maps of Daariya because the continent occupies more
than one passage condition.

Later officials select the politically convenient map and declare all others
forgeries.

### Rasen Ground

The agricultural rings, foundries and climate terraces. Rasens construct the
visible surface of the civilisation over older pressure systems maintained by
Lower Passage communities.

Their buildings endure longest, so later archaeologists mistake endurance for
sole authorship.

### Svyatorus Accord

The signal fields, treaty halls and memory choirs. Its travelling speakers
carry agreements between communities that do not share a script or body form.

When imperial language law arrives, these translators are retrospectively
celebrated as guardians of linguistic purity.

## 4. The people already beneath Daariya

The Lower Passage communities include Naga water-keepers, Deep Scale houses,
tunnel maintenance bodies and refugees from Arey. They do not form one nation.

The first polar covenant recognises their routes. The public copy omits the
recognition clause and describes them as temporary guests in tunnels they
built.

Conflict grows around three incompatible ideas of ownership:

- the Fleet claims land through recorded arrival;
- surface settlements claim it through labour and habitation;
- Lower Passage communities claim responsibility for load, water and the
  consequences of collapse.

None is simply correct. Only the Fleet possesses a ministry authorised to
print maps.

## 5. Asgard Iriysky

Asgard Iriysky begins as a southern exchange settlement at the joining of the
Iriy and Om waterways. It links Daariya with the continental lands collectively
called Rassenia.

It contains:

- an interworld landing court;
- the Perun Inspection Hall;
- the Nine-Book solar archive;
- a lunar traffic office;
- markets shared with Lower Passage communities;
- the Hall of Simultaneous Dates, where every delegation records the founding
  according to its own calendar.

Later chroniclers turn Asgard into the single capital of a planet-wide empire.
Surviving tax ledgers show that its authority ended wherever couriers refused
to travel in winter.

### The impossible founding date

The official date is calculated backward from a rare alignment of three moons.
Each revision improves its precision while using a different reconstruction of
Fatta's orbit.

The joke is not that ancient people cannot count. It is that modern certainty
increases as the surviving evidence decreases.

## 6. Perun's visit

Perun arrives in a Vaitmana to inspect the Golden Path and the lunar anchors.
His technical briefing covers:

- compression storms advancing from Peklo;
- copied identities passing through damaged stations;
- the danger of turning medical classifications into enemy races;
- faults in Lelya's water-control lattice;
- the need for independent evacuation routes.

The Inspection Hall records the briefing as questions, tolerances and disputed
readings. The later **Santias of Perun** preserve only Perun's answers and add
questions that make every answer prophetic.

Perun leaves no eternal command to hate inhabitants of Peklo. That teaching is
inserted after the Lelya war by an editor whose monastery receives funding from
the lunar defence office.

## 7. Dazhbog's lunar covenant

Tarkh Dazhbog administers the relationship between Midgard, its sun and its
three moons. His Nine Books are operating covenants, not holy scripture:

1. solar measure;
2. tidal balance;
3. passage geometry;
4. water exchange;
5. refuge law;
6. quarantine limits;
7. orbital intervention;
8. evacuation duty;
9. revocation and public accounting.

The ninth book states that no celestial intervention is sacred merely because
a god performs it. Later editions omit this book and call the remaining eight
the complete Nine.

## 8. Lelya before the war

Lelya is a small inhabited orbital station grown around an older moon. Its
interior contains reservoirs, passage machinery, civilian chambers and sealed
Koshchei capsules.

The word **Koshchei** originally names orders whose lives are stored outside
their current bodies in egg-shaped continuity vessels. It is a profession and
technology, not a species.

Lelya's population includes:

- lunar engineers;
- Koshchei continuity orders;
- Peklo refugees held in quarantine;
- copied persons employed as hazardous-environment workers;
- Fleet soldiers;
- families maintaining the reservoirs;
- a faction attempting to seize the passage anchor.

After communications fail, Daariya's defence office classifies every remaining
inhabitant as a hostile Koshchei.

## 9. The occupation

The **Sealed Egg faction** captures Lelya's central passage controls. Its exact
composition is disputed. Evidence supports several participants:

- Koshchei officers seeking permanent rule;
- Grey mimetic cells escaping quarantine;
- Fleet commanders planning emergency martial law;
- lunar workers who believe Daariya is draining their water;
- an unknown signal arriving through a compressed Peklo passage.

The faction threatens to redirect Lelya's mass and water lattice against
Midgard. It is a real danger. The claim that every lunar inhabitant serves it
is not.

### Propaganda compression

Official history performs four substitutions:

1. a coalition becomes one people;
2. one people becomes one race;
3. one military plan becomes their eternal nature;
4. killing civilians becomes impossible because the race is defined as
   non-civilian.

This mechanism is a principal target of the game's satire.

## 10. Spas and the forbidden forecast

**Spas** is the title of the chief disaster forecaster attached to the Svyatorus
Accord. The person holding it during the crisis is **Veda Spasova**, a practical
engineer of oral models who can calculate consequences when written systems no
longer agree.

She determines that destroying Lelya will:

- release its reservoirs;
- scatter fragments across the northern hemisphere;
- overload the polar pressure routes;
- destabilise Midgard's rotational balance;
- drown Daariya even if the strike defeats the Sealed Egg faction.

She does not foresee the future mystically. She compares nine incompatible
models and notices that every model predicts the same flood.

The Lunar Defence Council seals her warning to prevent panic. It then cites the
absence of public warning as evidence that no reliable forecast existed.

## 11. The Great Passage from Daariya

Veda Spasova begins evacuation without full authorisation. The four districts
move south along different routes:

- Da'Aryan carriers transport archives and seed ecologies;
- Kh'Aryan schools preserve maps, including mutually contradictory ones;
- Rasen crews build moving ground and repair the Riphean road;
- Svyatorus speakers keep the fragmented columns in treaty contact;
- Naga keepers open flooded routes that the surface maps deny exist;
- Arey survivors reinforce underground shelters.

This is the greatest cooperative act of the age. Later purity chronicles erase
the Lower Passage contribution and describe the migration as four immaculate
lineages marching alone.

The later sacred word **Paskhet** is claimed to preserve a complete sentence
about the path of the Aesir. Within canon, this is a folk etymology invented
centuries later. It becomes popular because it turns a chaotic refugee retreat
into a planned triumph.

## 12. Dazhbog's decision

Dazhbog faces three options:

1. negotiate while the passage anchor continues to destabilise;
2. capture the moon at enormous immediate cost;
3. destroy Lelya before it can be redirected against Midgard.

The Defence Council hides the scale of the evacuation from him and inflates
the estimate of hostile control. Dazhbog chooses destruction.

He is neither an infallible saviour nor a secret monster. He makes a defensible
decision using manipulated evidence, then accepts a title that conceals the
victims.

This is his lasting tragedy: he truly prevents one catastrophe and becomes the
author of another.

## 13. The breaking of Lelya

Dazhbog uses the solar covenant to force incompatible orbital instructions
through Lelya's passage anchor. The moon does not explode like a bomb. Its
layers cease agreeing about where the moon is.

The result appears as a flower of separated cardboard strata:

- outer route plates shear away;
- reservoirs become rings of ice and water;
- inhabited chambers fall along different trajectories;
- continuity eggs enter Midgard's Nav;
- the central anchor closes around the Sealed Egg command.

The familiar later image of a weapon striking an egg is a simplified ritual
diagram of this event. It is not the origin of every painted-egg custom in real
history.

## 14. The first great flood

Lelya's water and debris strike the polar region. The ancient pressure routes
carry the shock beneath Daariya. Four failures occur together:

1. coastal impact and atmospheric heating;
2. release of lunar reservoirs;
3. collapse of the Northern Well;
4. failure of overloaded subterranean water agreements.

Daariya breaks into moving plates and sinks beneath a new polar sea. Midgard's
axis and climate shift, but not by the impossible values claimed in later
school diagrams.

The flood is planetary in consequence, not literally uniform in depth. Many
regions survive, which is why every later culture possesses both survivors and
someone claiming that only their ancestors survived.

## 15. What survives

The evacuation preserves:

- portions of the Nine Books;
- four incompatible maps of Daariya;
- Lelya continuity eggs stranded in Nav;
- testimonies of lunar civilians;
- the public heroic chronicle;
- Veda Spasova's sealed forecast;
- a Lower Passage record proving that the flood followed ignored load limits;
- a fragment of Dazhbog's private admission of uncertainty.

No single source contains the whole event.

## 16. The official story

The post-flood state teaches:

> The alien Koshchei captured Lelya to destroy Midgard. Spas miraculously warned
> the pure clans. Dazhbog shattered the enemy moon, the Aesir followed the
> ordained path, and the flood proved both the wickedness of the foe and the
> favour of the gods.

Every part contains a trace of truth. Their arrangement removes responsibility:

- the coalition disappears into an alien race;
- engineering becomes prophecy;
- evacuation becomes a ceremonial march;
- preventable deaths become divine necessity;
- manipulated intelligence disappears from Dazhbog's decision.

## 17. Second-age factions

### The Four-District Assembly

Daariya's public government. Cooperative in crisis, paralysed by disputes over
which lineage arrived first.

### Lunar Defence Council

Military administrators who classify uncertainty as a security threat. They
survive the flood by controlling the official archive.

### Lelya Free Chambers

Lunar civilians and engineers resisting both the Sealed Egg faction and total
destruction. Their last transmission is removed from the heroic chronicle.

### The Sealed Egg

A real hostile coalition seeking control of the lunar anchor. Later history
inflates it into a timeless species.

### Spasova's Passage

Forecasters, translators and route-builders who organise evacuation across
lineage and species boundaries.

### The Lower Water Covenant

Naga and Deep Scale route communities whose infrastructure saves millions and
is blamed for the flood after the surface collapses.

### Dazhbog's Solar Office

Orbital engineers loyal to the Nine Books. It divides between those demanding
public accounting and those protecting the saviour legend.

## 18. Gameable dramatic arc

### Movement 1 — The radiant homeland

Build and defend Daariya under three differently moving moons. Learn the four
districts through useful cooperation rather than exposition.

### Movement 2 — The perfect capital

Open Asgard's date hall, inspect Perun's contradictory records and discover the
omitted ninth book.

### Movement 3 — Enemy on Lelya

Repel genuine attacks while the targeting system increasingly labels civilians,
refugees and maintenance bodies as Koshchei.

### Movement 4 — The warning

Help Veda Spasova prove the flood across conflicting models. The player cannot
prevent the strike but can determine who receives the warning.

### Movement 5 — The Great Passage

Hold evacuation roads, negotiate Lower Passage access and choose between
archives, people and machines as Daariya fails.

### Movement 6 — The breaking moon

Witness Dazhbog's decision from several incomplete perspectives. Rescue falling
Lelya chambers while fighting the Sealed Egg command.

### Movement 7 — Who writes the flood

After survival, decide which records remain separate and which are compressed
into a usable founding myth. There is no option to recover one perfect truth.

## 19. Mission and object seeds

- Three-Moon Orrery: displays different calendars without declaring one false.
- Lower Water Gate: opens only when surface and Naga route claims are both
  acknowledged.
- Perun Inspection Table: compares the briefing with later sacred questions.
- Ninth-Book Press: restores revocation clauses erased from public copies.
- Spas Choir: runs forecasts by combining partial oral models.
- Refuge Registry: can preserve individual identities or compress them into
  enemy classes.
- Lelya Continuity Egg: contains a person, a weapon command or both.
- Falling-Moon Map: changes terrain while remaining exact top-down gameplay.
- Archive Sled: forces a choice about which evidence travels south.

These are lore verbs and dramatic mechanisms, not inherited towers or weapons.
Their later gameplay implementation must preserve the distinction.

## 20. Visual foundation

- Daariya is a circular polar punchboard continent divided by four water cuts,
  visibly layered over older serpentine pressure routes.
- The three moons are separate suspended card mechanisms with distinct orbit
  rhythms, not identical glowing spheres.
- Asgard uses accumulated civic cards, date wheels and exchange bridges rather
  than monumental pseudo-Slavic fantasy architecture.
- Lelya is an inhabited layered utility moon with reservoirs and service
  chambers; it must look valuable before it looks threatening.
- The flood is shown through displaced terrain layers, swollen silver-blue
  paper channels and torn registration marks.
- Dazhbog's intervention uses solar-gold agreement strips forcing moon layers
  apart, not a generic laser beam.
- Evacuation columns visibly mix all lineages and Lower Passage peoples.

Exact top-down orthographic 90-degree projection remains mandatory for gameplay
objects. No swastika-derived ornament, supremacist colour coding, glossy space
opera or generic Viking architecture.

## 21. Canon boundaries

Canon inside the fictional world:

- Daariya existed and was a major polar civilisation;
- Midgard had three moons during this age;
- Lelya was inhabited and partially seized by a hostile coalition;
- Spasova forecast the consequences through engineering;
- the Defence Council suppressed and manipulated information;
- Dazhbog destroyed Lelya and prevented an immediate attack;
- the destruction caused the flood that ended Daariya;
- Lower Passage communities were essential to the evacuation;
- the official heroic account contains true events arranged to erase
  responsibility.

Not canon:

- Daariya as the origin of any real modern ethnicity;
- white or Indo-Aryan racial supremacy;
- all Koshchei, Greys or serpent peoples as one evil race;
- a real holiday as proof of a cosmic evacuation;
- real flood traditions as degraded copies of this single event;
- gods as automatically infallible;
- one chronicle possessing complete objective history.

## 22. Bridge to the third age

The survivors establish competing Rassenian settlements. The next age must
cover:

- the fragmentation of the four civic lineages into hereditary estates;
- refugees from Arey and the Deya system;
- the transfer and political meaning of Fatta;
- the rise of the Ants and Antlan;
- priests converting disaster memory into authority;
- gravitational, tectonic and nuclear weapons;
- the civil war that destroys Fatta and sinks Antlan;
- the second rewriting of a technological catastrophe as a war against an
  external Dark race.
