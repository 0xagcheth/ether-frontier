## 2. What to take from Andrboll

### Structural inspiration

1. **Knowing versus believing.**  
   The player constantly separates a living tradition, an honest uncertainty
   and a useful fiction from a coercive single answer.

2. **The counterfeit can become operational.**  
   A historically false text can still change people, institutions and the game
   world if enough names and actions are attached to it.

3. **Buried layers are interpretive, not merely archaeological.**  
   A buried city may be a real ruin, a later repair, a propaganda reconstruction
   and a memory of a different catastrophe at the same time.

4. **Humour protects against total certainty.**  
   Dry archival comedy, absurd pseudo-etymology and contradictory annotations
   are part of the tone. Humour never targets an ethnicity as inherently lesser.

5. **The enemy is forced sameness.**  
   Greys benefit whenever a culture is reduced to one pure origin, one correct
   symbol, one race or one authorised history.

### Imagery that may be fictionalised

- northern lost civilisation → a fictional polar ruin network; **Daariya** may
  appear only as a provisional/disputed name because it carries racialised
  Slavic-Aryan alternative-history associations;
- drowned island civilisation → **Antlan**, not historical Atlantis;
- buried nineteenth-century-like layers → several catastrophes and deliberate
  sealing, not a factual global mud flood;
- several moons and celestial craft → broken orbital “moon cards” and ancestral
  routes inside the fictional cosmos;
- Greys → nonhuman metaphysics of erasure, never coded as Jews, Black people,
  migrants or another real group;
- lizard peoples → multiple Lower-Serpent cultures with their own interests and
  internal politics, not biological evil;
- Book of Veles → a contested group of fragments whose authenticity remains an
  in-world conflict;
- pseudo-etymology → a gameplay system of tempting false connections that may
  power a spell briefly but corrupt its later effect.

### Material that must not become game truth

- racial hierarchy, blood purity or “civilising” races;
- antisemitic conspiracy, “Zionist government” or replacement myths;
- dehumanisation of Black people or any contemporary population;
- Nazi or neo-Nazi symbolism;
- claims that one modern nation created every language, science or civilisation;
- denial of historical violence;
- real political enemies rewritten as literal nonhumans;
- copied sacred signs used as generic magic buttons.

## 3. Cultural-lane model

The former USSR was a political space containing many different cultures. It is
not a single ethnicity, religion or mythological system. Ether Frontier should
use **cultural lanes**: each lane owns particular narrative principles,
materials, performance forms and environment rhythms.

No lane is an enemy faction. No lane is reduced to costume decoration.

### A. East Slavic lane

Sources and motifs:

- three-part cosmos: sky, earth, lower world;
- Veles, Perun, Mokosh, Dazhbog, Stribog and lower spirits;
- Mother Moist Earth as living, fertile and inviolable ground;
- epic heroes, household and landscape spirits;
- seasonal death/renewal motifs associated with Mara/Marena in later and local
  traditions.

Game contribution:

- foundational language of contracts with soil, storm, wind and memory;
- Mother Earth as the battlefield's living substrate;
- an original Ether Frontier interpretation of Mara as necessary ending and
  release, explicitly distinguished from both documented regional folklore and
  Grey erasure;
- craft materials: timber, birch bark, stone, linen, forged clamps.

Boundary:

- do not invent a fully documented ancient unified pantheon;
- clearly distinguish attested folklore, scholarly reconstruction and modern
  neo-pagan synthesis.

### B. Volga-Ural Turkic lane: Tatars and Bashkirs

Sources and motifs:

- epic transmission and sung/recited narrative;
- `Ural-Batyr`, `Akbuzat`, regional variants of `Alpamysh`;
- water of life, mountains, winged horse, lower-world monsters and the hero's
  duty to community;
- Volga-Bulgar sacred geography and layered Muslim/local historiography;
- long song, short song, recitative, dumbyra and the authority of the storyteller.

Game contribution:

- mountain and water contracts;
- a defence family that changes through recited verses rather than a fixed tech
  tree;
- mounted/winged movement grammar and curved felt/leather/card construction;
- bosses whose defeat requires completing or correcting an epic sequence.

Boundary:

- Tatars and Bashkirs remain distinct cultures;
- Islamic history and living faith are not treated as decorative pagan fantasy;
- no direct copying of tamgas, mosque ornament or sacred calligraphy.

### C. Central Asian lane: Uzbeks, Karakalpaks, Kazakhs, Kyrgyz and neighbours

Sources and motifs:

- `Alpamysh/Alpomish`, `Manas` and other distinct epic cycles;
- bakhshi, akyn and manaschi performance;
- oral improvisation, memory, solidarity and communal judgement;
- atlas/adras ikat, felt, wood, ceramic, metal and mobile architecture;
- garden, desert, mountain and river-oasis spatial organisation.

Game contribution:

- song-driven support units whose effects change according to narrative order;
- vivid warp-dyed paper/fabric layers with blurred printed edges;
- portable defence modules and folding shelters;
- oasis maps where water routing is both economy and defence;
- heroes defined by promise, hospitality, return and restoration rather than
  conquest.

Boundary:

- Uzbek, Karakalpak, Kazakh, Kyrgyz, Tajik and Turkmen sources are never treated
  as interchangeable;
- Islamic, Persianate and Turkic layers must remain visible where relevant;
- no generic “oriental” palace or carpet fantasy.

### D. Finno-Ugric lane: Mari, Udmurt, Komi, Mordvin, Karelian and others

Sources and motifs:

- multiple distinct peoples and traditions;
- Mari creation stories involving the world ocean, diving bird, world tree and
  world mountain;
- traditions describing seven worlds;
- deep forest, river, animal, household and place relationships;
- carved and painted wood, birch bark, metal pendants and textile rhythm.

Game contribution:

- multi-layer world maps and vertical passage between seven domains;
- bird-diver mechanics that recover construction material from submerged
  memory;
- forests that are negotiating agents, not scenery;
- top-down rhythm based on branching, water rings and paired creator/opponent
  actions.

Boundary:

- “Finno-Ugric” is a language-family label, not one mythology;
- living community religion and sacred groves require special restraint;
- no direct reuse of ceremonial signs.

### E. Siberian and Far-Eastern lane

Sources and motifs:

- Yakut Olonkho and other distinct northern epic traditions;
- worlds connected by journeys of heroes, spirits and animals;
- survival in extreme landscapes and the narrator as cultural memory;
- taiga, permafrost, river, coast, fish skin, birch bark, bone, hide, wood and
  repaired twentieth-century infrastructure.

Game contribution:

- main environmental body of the current game;
- strong vertical world-tree/map logic;
- seasonal transformation of paths;
- animal and landscape spirits as independent allies;
- repair culture connecting traditional material knowledge with Soviet and
  post-Soviet remnants.

Boundary:

- do not use the word “shaman” as a generic class for every northern people;
- sacred drums, masks, clothing and ritual objects are not generic VFX props.

### F. Caucasus lane

Sources and motifs:

- Nart cycles across several distinct North Caucasian traditions;
- Armenian `Daredevils of Sassoun`;
- Georgian polyphonic and heroic traditions;
- Azerbaijani/Turkic `Dede Qorqud` traditions;
- towers, mountain paths, stone, metal, vine, music and local hospitality laws.

Game contribution:

- mountain-gate maps and unequal defence;
- polyphonic cooperative mechanics;
- oath, guest-right and family-memory mission rules;
- heavy interlocking stone-card architecture.

Boundary:

- never merge Armenian, Georgian, Azerbaijani and North Caucasian histories into
  one decorative mountain culture;
- avoid contemporary territorial conflicts and copied religious architecture.

### G. Baltic lane

Sources and motifs:

- multipart song traditions such as Lithuanian sutartinės;
- song/dance celebrations and communal continuity;
- straw constructions, wood, amber, flax and restrained geometric rhythm.

Game contribution:

- canon/round-based defence in which staggered voices create overlapping zones;
- lightweight straw-card celestial structures;
- amber and flax material families.

Boundary:

- Baltic traditions are not Slavic;
- crosses and living sacred symbols are not generic interface icons.

## 4. Revised cosmological proposal

This is an original game synthesis, not a recovered shared ancient cosmology.
`Yav/Nav/Prav` in particular must remain the terminology of one contested
in-world school rather than the objective map used by every culture.

The game should not assign every culture to a separate fantasy planet. The
shared world is connected by **roads**, while each culture retains a different
way of describing those roads.

Proposed compatible layers:

- **Yav** — manifested forms and material action;
- **Mother Earth** — living body carrying all surface cultures and buried
  memories;
- **Nav** — incomplete traces and uncompleted obligations;
- **Mara's Limit** — an original game law combining regional associations with
  death, nightmare, winter and ending; not a claim about one universal ancient
  goddess;
- **Iriy** — preserved names and return routes;
- **Lower Serpent domains** — real underground homelands, not identical to Nav;
- **Rule/Prav** — the negotiated constraints connecting worlds, not a heavenly
  bureaucracy;
- **Grey interval** — a non-place created when differences and names are
  removed.

Different peoples may name and interpret these layers differently. The game
never declares one modern ethnic vocabulary the secret original language of
all humanity.

## 5. Gameplay implications

### Knowing system

Every lore discovery is stored with three parallel fields:

1. **witness** — what a person or tradition says;
2. **evidence** — what the player can currently verify;
3. **use** — what action this interpretation enables.

Contradictory witnesses may coexist. A convenient falsehood can grant immediate
power but raise Grey influence.

### Oral memory

Some abilities are activated not by static runes but by sequences:

- sung line;
- answered line;
- instrumental rhythm;
- placement or movement;
- final communal confirmation.

This permits respectful inspiration from epic performance without copying exact
lyrics or sacred text.

### Mother Earth

- build cells have health and recovery;
- excessive extraction injures the battlefield;
- roots, water and stone remember previous placements;
- some missions are won by leaving ground unbuilt;
- terrain healing becomes a strategic resource.

### Mara

- safely ends repeating Nav loops;
- converts destroyed forms into reusable memory rather than raw currency;
- closes paths that should no longer remain open;
- opposes both uncontrolled resurrection and Grey deletion.

### Cultural allies

Allies are written as specific fictional communities inspired by a limited set
of principles. They are not direct representatives of modern ethnic groups and
do not speak for those groups.

## 6. Visual translation rules

1. Use material and construction before ornament.
2. Record the specific cultural lane behind every borrowed principle.
3. Never mix motifs simply because their peoples lived in one state.
4. Prefer craft processes: weaving, layering, riveting, carving, folding,
   knotting, felting, dyeing and recitation structure.
5. Do not copy a living sacred sign, garment or ritual object without explicit
   review.
6. Soviet/post-Soviet influence appears as shared historical experience:
   engineering clarity, public mosaics, printed manuals, repair culture,
   apartment and industrial materials, cosmic optimism, ruins and local reuse.
7. It never erases older or non-Russian cultural identity.
8. Every final prompt names its cultural lane, source principle, transformation
   and forbidden direct copies.
