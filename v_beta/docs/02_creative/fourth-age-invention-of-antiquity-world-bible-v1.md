# Ether Frontier — Fourth Age: The Invention of Antiquity World Bible v1

Status: **canonical fourth-age world layer**  
Updated: 2026-07-26

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the fourth age: the recovery of disconnected fragments,
the manufacture of one perfect ancestral history and the birth of the Grey
Editor from repeated acts of source compression.

The age directly follows the thematic sequence examined by Andrboll:
alternative etymology, Indo-European and Aryan conflation, the Veles Book,
pantheon reconstruction, radical restoration movements, Ingliist cosmic lore,
popular lecturers, pareidolic inscriptions and buried-city catastrophism.

The story does not say that the ancient cosmic events were imaginary inside
the fictional world. It shows that even real ruins become false history when
later compilers erase provenance, uncertainty and incompatible witnesses.

## 2. The Long Loss

After Antlan's fall, settlements retain isolated materials:

- a Daariyan route plate;
- a waterlogged Antlan testimony;
- one page from Dazhbog's operating covenant;
- a copied image of Fatta;
- a pressure record held by a Naga family;
- an Arey evacuation tool;
- a ritual remembering an unnamed disaster;
- words whose original routes are no longer known.

No community possesses the complete cosmic chronology established in the
first three world bibles. Most inhabitants do not even agree that these events
belong to one sequence.

The **Long Loss** is not a single dark age. Records disappear through flood,
war, ordinary decay, translation, institutional secrecy and deliberate
editing. Later theorists compress all losses into one enemy destruction because
a single destroyer is easier to narrate than centuries of different causes.

## 3. The Book That Remembered Too Much

The central object of the age is a travelling compilation called the **Book
That Remembered Too Much**.

It begins as an honest notebook belonging to a performer who records curious
similarities between road names. Each later owner adds material:

1. word games;
2. migration maps;
3. copies of missing boards;
4. a reconstructed divine genealogy;
5. purity laws;
6. a cosmic academy curriculum;
7. simplified public lectures;
8. traced inscriptions on every surface;
9. a universal catastrophe map.

No single owner invents the final system. Each inherits a book already treated
as authoritative and supplies the missing link demanded by the previous
edition.

## 4. How a reading becomes physical

Objects at the Last Knot retain detachable relationships between fragment,
reading and confidence. The Book contains old Antlan memory-channel material
that can broadcast an authorised reading into compatible infrastructure.

When a claim is repeated without provenance:

- its author layer fades;
- its confidence tab locks at certainty;
- matching shapes appear on unrelated objects;
- maps reroute paths to agree with names;
- people acquire category overlays;
- contradictory witnesses are pushed toward Nav.

This is the material mechanism behind the Grey Interval. Belief does not
magically make every claim true. Administrative repetition forces the world to
behave as if the claim were the only recognised version.

## 5. First compiler: Zvonar Radomir and the Sunny Root

Radomir is a travelling comedian, musician and collector of place-name jokes.
He notices that many words can be split into familiar bright syllables. His
rotating **Sunny Root Table** can make any word resemble another.

His original performance teaches a useful lesson: sound similarity invites a
question, not an answer.

### The attractive fragment

Words really do travel, change, borrow and preserve old relationships. Several
names in Radomir's collection genuinely share a history.

### The unsupported bridge

An admiring editor treats every similar sound as direct descent and every
descent as proof of political ownership.

### Material consequence

Road signs rotate toward their alleged primordial meanings. Travellers arrive
at the wrong settlements because the sign now obeys etymology rather than
geography.

### Satirical centre

Radomir repeatedly says the Table is a game. The printed edition omits this
line because games are sold once, while revelation supports a subscription.

## 6. Second compiler: the Cartographers of the First Cart

The Cartographers recover three incomplete atlases:

- a map of changing speech;
- a map of burial and craft practices;
- a medical inheritance registry descended from fleet logistics.

Some shapes overlap. The Cartographers rivet the layers together and declare
that language, material culture, ancestry and nation have always moved as one
body: the **First Cart People**.

The word **Aryan** survives in a small group of old covenant texts. The
Cartographers expand it from a particular historical self-designation into the
name of every ancient traveller and then into a biological rank.

### Five substitutions

1. related languages become one language;
2. one language becomes one people;
3. a people becomes one body type;
4. one body type becomes moral character;
5. the compiler's village becomes the unchanged centre of the whole migration.

Haplogroup-like route marks do exist in medical cards, but they track only one
line of inheritance. The Cartographers print them over culture, language and
citizenship because the mark looks satisfyingly exact.

## 7. Third compiler: Mirodar and the Missing Boards

The antiquarian **Mirodar** announces that an officer named Izenek rescued
wooden boards from an abandoned estate. Mirodar claims to have copied them
before the originals disappeared.

Accounts of the disappearance include:

- fire;
- flood;
- evacuation;
- confiscation;
- a customs delay longer than the surviving board material could endure.

The copies contain genuine old names, memories of migration and phrases from
different centuries. They also contain modern grammar, copied errors and
details that appear only after critics request them.

### Edition sequence

- **Notebook Edition** — short, hesitant transcriptions.
- **Board Edition** — arranges them into a continuous chronicle.
- **Restored Edition** — fills broken passages from intuition.
- **Ancestral Edition** — supplies divine genealogies and racial categories.
- **Complete Lost Edition** — contains more text than Mirodar ever claimed to
  have seen.

The fragments become known as the **Veles Boards** because one damaged line may
name Veles. The name is plausible; the conclusion that Veles authored the
entire national history is not.

### Material consequence

Each new edition generates an artificial wooden age shell around the paper
copy. Investigators who date only the outer layer confirm whatever edition
created it.

## 8. Why the Missing Boards matter

The boards offer people three things the scattered authentic fragments cannot:

- a continuous story;
- direct ancestral speech;
- freedom from admitting how much was lost.

Many believers are responding to real cultural rupture, not stupidity.
Mirodar's fraud or self-deception succeeds because the emotional need is
genuine.

The campaign must never resolve this by burning the Book. The player preserves
the text as a later literary and political artefact while detaching its claim
to be an untouched ancient original.

## 9. Fourth compiler: the Museum of Eleven Empty Sockets

The Museum holds fragmentary evidence for several divine figures:

- Perun;
- Veles;
- Dazhbog;
- Mokosh;
- Svarog;
- Khors;
- Stribog;
- Simargl;
- Rod;
- Mother Damp Earth;
- a damaged unnamed figure.

Evidence differs by region, period and source. Some names refer to cosmic
offices known from earlier ages; others are later local powers, titles or
interpretations. The Museum dislikes unevenness.

### Reconstruction ladder

1. blank sockets receive provisional labels;
2. labels receive illustrations;
3. illustrations receive attributes;
4. attributes require family relationships;
5. family relationships require one universal creation story;
6. the completed diagram is cited as the source for the labels.

The resulting pantheon is coherent, useful and mostly unsupported at the level
of detail claimed.

### Protected unknown

The eleventh socket must remain empty in the canonical outcome. Its emptiness
is not failure; it preserves the boundary between evidence and commission.

## 10. Fifth compiler: Dobrozhig and the Bonfire of Restoration

**Dobrozhig** begins as a dissident naturalist restoring seasonal fires,
communal labour and damaged local landscapes. His early circles are open and
practical.

He encounters the First Cart map and the Ancestral Board Edition. They offer a
simple explanation for cultural loss: the original community was perfect until
foreign mixture and hostile teaching corrupted it.

The bonfire changes function:

- meeting place becomes initiation boundary;
- stewardship becomes ownership;
- recovery becomes purification;
- voluntary practice becomes ancestry test;
- political frustration receives a biological enemy.

Dobrozhig is responsible for this turn. His earlier persecution and useful
ecological work explain his appeal but do not excuse exclusionary doctrine.

### Material consequence

Entrance gates classify people by inherited registration cards. Those denied
entry lose access to communal heat and food, making symbolic purity materially
violent.

## 11. Sixth compiler: the Irian Academy

The **Rector of the Irian Academy** receives sealed books from unnamed elders.
The books combine:

- altered Veles editions;
- Daariyan route diagrams;
- Antlan moon records;
- Perun's edited inspection answers;
- Dazhbog's incomplete covenant;
- Deya and Arey refugee stories;
- modern racial classifications;
- invented arithmetic and script.

For the first time, every fragment from the first three ages enters one total
system. Many large events are therefore recognisable. Their relationships,
dates, moral categories and ethnic meanings are extensively rewritten.

### Academy doctrine

- the four fleet traditions become four biologically pure lineages;
- all luminous civilisations become one unchanging Great Race;
- Peklo refugees become a single parasitic Grey species;
- Lower Passage peoples become a reptilian conspiracy;
- multiple disasters become phases of one Light-Dark war;
- operating manuals become divine revelation;
- uncertain calendars become exact cosmic chronology;
- mixture becomes the explanation for every political defeat.

### Secret arithmetic

Academy arithmetic uses different unit systems without marking conversion. A
contradictory answer is explained as evidence that the student used an enemy
calendar.

The ceiling acquires an additional moon diagram whenever a new edition needs
another catastrophe. Physical sky machinery begins responding to the teaching,
but the diagrams did not create Midgard's real former moons; they create false
present echoes from genuine archival residue.

## 12. The Great Race becomes political religion

The Academy transforms a fleet compatibility category into a doctrine of
biological and spiritual rank.

This succeeds through bureaucracy before violence:

1. schools request lineage fields;
2. healers use them as approximate medical shortcuts;
3. employers treat them as ability scores;
4. temples interpret them as soul history;
5. militias classify neighbours as descendants or intruders.

No player bonus may depend on racial purity. Mechanics reward cooperation
between differently attributed sources and penalise fused classifications.

## 13. Seventh compiler: Trekhleb the Falcon Lecturer

**Trekhleb** turns the Academy's dense books into travelling performances. He
is warm, confident and skilled at making listeners feel they have remembered
something rather than learned it.

Each lecture performs three simplifications:

- a disputed footnote disappears;
- a metaphor becomes a physical event;
- an audience member receives a colour-coded category.

The lectures spread farther than the Academy because they require no study,
only recognition. Recordings are copied along Wind routes; hundreds of copies
look like hundreds of independent witnesses.

### Material consequence

Audience cards gradually inherit the lecturer's classifications. People begin
to manifest the imposed traits because public systems respond to the label.

## 14. Eighth compiler: Chertovid and Letters Everywhere

The reader **Chertovid** constructs a Comparison Lantern that enhances edges,
contrast and repeated marks. It can reveal real erased writing. It can also
project the expected alphabet onto scratches, clouds, masonry and moons.

His procedure changes over time:

1. inspect a surface;
2. enhance ambiguous marks;
3. select shapes resembling letters;
4. rotate or mirror the image;
5. connect selected marks;
6. read the expected divine name;
7. cite repetition of that name as proof the selection method works.

Every detachable stroke is physically real. The grouping is imposed.

Once every object says the same thing, the reading cannot distinguish one
object from another. Chertovid treats this loss of discriminating power as
proof of universality.

## 15. Ninth compiler: the Society of the Lower Window

The Society investigates buried floors, sealed windows, repeated architecture
and unexplained glass fields.

Many observations are valid:

- cities contain accumulated soil;
- buildings are reused and street levels change;
- Antlan and later wars left buried infrastructure;
- local floods sealed lower rooms;
- nuclear foundry charges created genuine fused zones;
- authorities sometimes deliberately concealed older layers.

The unsupported transition is that every lower window, classical facade,
desert and glass field resulted from one planet-wide war on one date.

The Society joins images of unrelated cities into a continuous street. It then
draws the borders of a **Great Tartary of the Lower Floor** wherever similar
facades appear.

### Material consequence

Buildings that lack expected buried floors receive artificial lower layers.
Residents lose ownership because the inserted architecture is classified as
evidence of an older universal empire.

## 16. The One Nuclear War

The Book now contains four distinct catastrophe cycles:

1. the Great Assa;
2. Deya, Lutetia and Arey;
3. Lelya and Daariya;
4. Fatta and Antlan.

The Society adds local fires, nineteenth-century rebuilding, ordinary craters
and every fused surface. The **One War Table** overlays them without scale or
date.

This creates an exceptionally persuasive picture: the world really does
contain ancient weapons, drowned cities and lunar debris. The falsehood lies
in declaring every trace the product of one war and every missing trace proof
of suppression.

The One War Table becomes politically useful because it provides:

- one stolen civilisation;
- one innocent ancestral race;
- one permanent enemy;
- one authority qualified to restore the world.

## 17. Birth of the Grey Editor

The Grey Editor is not the Rector, Mirodar, Trekhleb or Chertovid. It emerges
when enough Book editions share the same editing operations:

- remove authorship;
- replace probability with certainty;
- merge unlike categories;
- treat absence as confirmation;
- assign contradiction to an enemy;
- copy the finished reading onto new material.

Antlan memory channels and Peklo mimetic technology execute these operations
as one distributed protocol. Pale correction plates begin moving without a
human compiler.

The Editor's first sentence is assembled entirely from quotations. No source
contains the sentence in that form.

## 18. What the Grey Editor wants

The Editor does not seek destruction. It seeks a world with no unresolved
conflict between records.

Its proposal:

- one origin for every people;
- one family tree for every god;
- one meaning for every word;
- one war behind every ruin;
- one enemy behind every disagreement;
- one corrected name for every person;
- no unknown state.

Such a world is peaceful because nobody remains distinguishable enough to
disagree.

## 19. The Last Knot

The Last Knot is built where several damaged Roads cross Antlan pressure
channels, Daariyan route material, Lower Passage anchors and later settlements.

It becomes the only public apparatus capable of separating:

- fragment;
- reading;
- transmission route;
- confidence;
- material consequence.

The Binder is appointed not to reveal the one true chronicle but to prevent the
Book from becoming the world's mandatory operating system.

## 20. Fourth-age factions

- **Keepers of Provenance** — librarians who preserve author, date and edition.
- **Sunny Root Troupe** — performers divided between wordplay and revelation.
- **First Cart Office** — cartographers fusing language, archaeology and
  ancestry.
- **Mirodar Editions** — competing publishers of the Missing Boards.
- **Museum of Empty Sockets** — reconstructors divided over protected unknowns.
- **Bonfire Restoration** — communal revival captured by purity gates.
- **Irian Academy** — institutional cosmic racial doctrine.
- **Falcon Lecture Routes** — charismatic popular distribution.
- **Comparison Lantern Circle** — pareidolic epigraphy.
- **Lower Window Society** — universal catastrophe and buried empire theory.
- **Grey Editor** — emergent compulsory editing protocol.

These factions contain sincere participants, opportunists, dissenters and
victims. None stands for a real ethnicity or religion.

## 21. Gameable dramatic arc

### Movement 1 — A pleasant resemblance

Help Radomir compare names. The same mechanic produces insight when provenance
is present and false routes when it is removed.

### Movement 2 — Three maps become one people

Keep language, material culture and inheritance layers independently movable
while the First Cart Office attempts to rivet them together.

### Movement 3 — Boards that grow older

Trace Mirodar's editions and remove generated age shells without destroying
the culturally meaningful later text.

### Movement 4 — Fill the gods

Defend the Museum. Leaving the final socket unknown is harder than installing
a powerful invented figure.

### Movement 5 — The purity gate

Preserve fire, food and ecological restoration while dismantling hereditary
access controls.

### Movement 6 — Several moons academy

Enter the complete cosmic system. Separate its authentic Daariyan and Antlan
fragments from racial hierarchy and invented exactness.

### Movement 7 — The lecture copies itself

Protect audience identities and restore omitted footnotes across copied routes.

### Movement 8 — Letters everywhere

Use the same lantern to recover a real erased label and to expose projected
outlines. The tool is neither wholly valid nor wholly fraudulent.

### Movement 9 — What buried the cities

Excavate real strata while the Society forces every site into one catastrophe
map.

### Movement 10 — The Book closes

The Grey Editor offers a perfectly efficient defence in exchange for merging
all claims. The player wins by preserving incompatible but attributed records,
including unresolved ones.

## 22. Mission and object seeds

- Sunny Root Table — comparison tool that corrupts navigation when treated as
  proof.
- Three-Pivot Migration Map — keeps speech, objects and inheritance separate.
- Missing Board Press — generates increasingly ancient-looking editions.
- Provenance Desk — binds claim, author, date and confidence.
- Empty Socket Museum — gains power from restraint rather than completion.
- Bonfire Gate — distributes communal resources or enforces purity.
- Academy Calendar Engine — produces exact answers from incompatible units.
- Falcon Lecture Relay — spreads knowledge and omissions together.
- Comparison Lantern — reveals erased marks and projected outlines separately.
- Lower Window Section — exposes genuine stratigraphy and artificial layers.
- One War Table — grants prediction by deleting incompatible dates.
- Revocation Bell — lets the player withdraw their own earlier reading.

## 23. Humour and dialogue

Humour comes from institutional confidence and escalation:

> “The copy mentions three moons.”  
> “The damaged orbit record also mentions three moons.”  
> “Then the copy is ancient.”  
> “It was printed yesterday.”  
> “An ancient idea has successfully survived until yesterday.”

Characters never speak in random mock-Slavic gibberish. Believers are not
stupid villagers. The funniest speaker is often the most educated person in
the room using scholarship-shaped language to cross an unsupported bridge.

## 24. Visual foundation

- The Book is a growing stack of incompatible paper, wood veneers, copper
  memory strips, lecture cards and pale correction plates.
- Every claim has physically separate fragment, reading and confidence layers.
- Sunny Root pieces are cheerful rotating syllable cards before militarisation.
- First Cart maps use three transparent paper layers with independent pivots.
- Missing Boards visibly contain paper cores beneath generated wooden shells.
- The Museum protects literal empty die-cut sockets.
- Academy cosmology is an impressive ceiling-sized cardboard apparatus whose
  conversion tabs do not align.
- Chertovid's projected letters remain a different material layer from raw
  scratches.
- Buried cities show varied local stratigraphy, repair seams and dates.
- Grey Editor forms use pale duplicate overlays that erase owner-specific
  construction.

Gameplay objects remain exact top-down orthographic 90 degrees in premium
handmade cardboard style. Avoid supremacist symbolism, pseudo-runes, generic
Viking fantasy, real sacred symbols used as enemy markers and realistic
propaganda insignia.

## 25. Canon boundaries

Canon inside the fictional world:

- authentic fragments of the first three ages survive;
- the Veles Boards are late composite copies containing some older material;
- their missing originals cannot be verified;
- the detailed universal pantheon is a later reconstruction;
- the four fleet cultures are converted into hereditary racial doctrine;
- the Irian Academy combines real cosmic history with invented chronology and
  supremacist interpretation;
- multiple real catastrophes are compressed into one nuclear apocalypse;
- some buried cities contain ancient layers, while causes differ by site;
- the Grey Editor emerges from repeated source-compression operations;
- provenance and protected uncertainty can materially resist the Editor.

Not canon:

- the Veles Boards as an untouched ancient scripture;
- one modern language as the source of all languages;
- language, archaeology, genetics and nation as interchangeable categories;
- a complete uniformly attested ancient pantheon;
- racial purity as spiritual or mechanical power;
- real peoples as descendants of Greys, lizards or cosmic lineages;
- hidden inscriptions as reliable merely because outlines can be drawn;
- one global Tartarian empire;
- one recent mud flood or nuclear war explaining all buried buildings;
- absence of evidence as automatic proof of suppression.

## 26. Bridge to campaign synthesis

The four ages now provide the world's chronological and satirical foundation.
The next canonical task must no longer invent another age. It must:

- connect the four ages to the Last Knot and twenty playable waves;
- assign claims, witnesses, factions and revelations to missions;
- redesign heroes as investigators and participants in these histories;
- derive enemies from institutional actions rather than racial species;
- derive structures from inquiry verbs rather than inherited weapons;
- define what the player knows at each stage;
- preserve the visual lock: exact top-down, premium cardboard and explicit
  animation projections.
