# Ether Frontier — Four Ages Campaign Synthesis v1

Status: **canonical narrative campaign target; runtime migration required**  
Updated: 2026-07-26

## 1. Purpose

This document converts the four canonical world ages into one playable
twenty-wave campaign on the current Last Knot board.

It owns:

- the order in which the player learns the world;
- the meaning of all twenty waves;
- the narrative identity of heroes, hostile formations and bosses;
- the role of the seven buildable inquiry objects;
- the relationship between campaign truth and player-visible claims;
- the ending after wave 20.

It does not claim that the current runtime already implements every new lore
verb, dialogue scene, state change or ending. Existing timings, paths, economy,
enemy categories and stable IDs are compatibility constraints. Old weapons,
abilities and identities are not creative constraints.

## 2. Campaign premise

The Last Knot has begun receiving pulses from the Grey Interval. Each pulse
contains bodies, machines, memories and historical fragments compressed into a
single hostile formation.

Rada Sveden believes the **Book That Remembered Too Much** can predict the
pulses. It does. Every successful prediction also teaches the Knot to classify
more unrelated beings as the same enemy.

The Binder must defend the Knot while reconstructing four historical ages:

1. arrival on Midgard and invention of the Great Race;
2. Daariya, Lelya and the first flood;
3. Antlan, Fatta and the civil nuclear war;
4. creation of the total pseudo-history and birth of the Grey Editor.

The campaign is therefore both defence and investigation. Destroying a carrier
stops an immediate breach. Understanding why it was classified as an enemy
prevents the Editor from generating the same category again.

## 3. What the player knows

### At the opening

- a dangerous Grey force is approaching;
- the Book predicts its shapes;
- serpent, Nav and machine bodies appear among the attackers;
- the Last Knot must survive.

### What the player does not know

- Greys are not one biological race;
- several serpent communities were coerced or misclassified;
- the four Great Race lineages began as fleet cultures;
- Midgard was inhabited before the First Landing;
- Dazhbog caused the Daariyan flood after receiving manipulated evidence;
- Antlan destroyed itself through civil war;
- the Book is a layered modern compilation;
- the Grey Editor is an editing protocol created by the Book's operations.

## 4. Structure of the twenty waves

| Chapter | Waves | Historical layer | Runtime milestone |
|---|---:|---|---|
| I. The Attractive Book | 1–4 | Fourth Age surface | tutorial, economy and first raids |
| II. Beneath the Empty World | 5–9 | First Cosmic Age | first boss, air and Nav pressure |
| III. Three Moons | 10–14 | Daariya and Lelya | second Road and captured machines |
| IV. Corrected Heaven | 15–17 | Antlan and Fatta | sky boss and elite mixed pressure |
| V. One History for Everything | 18–20 | Fourth Age revealed | simultaneous Roads and final engine |

The player encounters the ages in investigative order, not chronology. The
Book's newest claims appear first; deeper waves expose the older fragments from
which those claims were built.

## 5. Chapter I — The Attractive Book

### Wave 1 — A Pleasant Resemblance

Runtime compatibility: `fast`, `warrior`; one Road.

Visible event: Radomir's Sunny Root Table correctly predicts the first runners
by matching a repeated route syllable.

Claim offered: “The same root means the same people use the same Road.”

Primary trace: two similar names and one genuine copied sign.

Player lesson: comparison is useful; descent remains unproven.

Required object introduction: **Wind Route Table**.

After-wave reveal: the prediction succeeded because both signs were copied
from one recent route ledger, not because the syllable is primordial.

### Wave 2 — Three Maps

Runtime compatibility: first `brute`.

Visible event: the First Cart Office overlays speech, craft and inheritance
maps to predict a heavy formation.

Claim offered: “One overlap proves one unchanged ancestral nation.”

Player action: rotate map layers independently. The local overlap remains
useful without fusing all three maps.

Required object introduction: **Stone Load Chorus**.

### Wave 3 — The Missing Boards

Runtime compatibility: first `raider`; roadside structures at risk.

Visible event: Name Hooks remove edition tabs from Mirodar's copies. A new
wooden shell makes yesterday's page appear ancient.

Claim offered: “Only enemies of memory ask to see the original.”

Player action: protect the paper copy, provenance tab and claimed original as
three different states.

Required object introduction: **Name Witness Table**.

### Wave 4 — The Price of Certainty

Runtime compatibility: larger mixed ground pulse; economy test.

Visible event: completing every missing sentence grants the Book a perfect
prediction but causes the Knot to misidentify friendly workers.

Claim offered: “An incomplete history cannot defend anyone.”

Player action: leave at least one confidence tab at **unknown**.

Required object introduction: **Root Return Nursery**.

Chapter revelation: the Book is useful precisely because it contains real
fragments. Usefulness does not authenticate its publication story.

## 6. Chapter II — Beneath the Empty World

### Wave 5 — Voivode of the Red Plate

Runtime compatibility: `miniBossGround`, `fast`, `raider`, `brute`.

Boss identity: **Voivode of the Red Plate**, commander bound by a disputed
Lower Passage debt. Red scale is an oath layer, not a racial trait.

Visible event: the Book calls every serpent body an ancient enemy.

Counter-witness: Sarma produces two versions of the polar landing covenant.

Boss resolution: expose and detach the military oath. Defeating the body alone
stops the wave; resolving the debt unlocks Sarma's testimony.

Hero recruitment becomes available after this wave.

### Wave 6 — The Sky Does Not Touch the Map

Runtime compatibility: first `air` pressure.

Historical fragment: Da'Aryan descent routes and Kh'Aryan dimensional weather.

False reading: fleet arrival proves ownership of an empty Midgard.

Counter-evidence: aerial bearings cross water routes maintained before the
landing.

Required object introduction: **Sun Comparison Measure**.

### Wave 7 — Four Lineages

Runtime compatibility: mixed ground, air and raiders.

The Book assigns every formation to one of four hereditary colours. The player
finds mixed crews using all four technical traditions.

Protected conclusion: Da'Aryan, Kh'Aryan, Rasen and Svyatorus identities were
fleet and civic cultures later made hereditary.

### Wave 8 — Unfinished Passage

Runtime compatibility: first `wraith`/Nav formations.

The Book classifies Nav witnesses and copied Peklo refugees as dead Greys.
Mara demonstrates that an unfinished form, a copied body and a completed death
require different endings.

Required object introduction: **Water Exchange Weir**.

### Wave 9 — The First Missing Record

Runtime compatibility: largest single-Road mixed test.

Historical reconstruction: the Fleet found earlier inhabitants and sealed the
evidence in the Arleg Archive.

Moral turn: the civilisation that defined itself as guardian of memory began
with an act of historical erasure.

Chapter revelation: the Great Race was neither imaginary nor racially pure. It
was a real coalition whose later identity doctrine falsified its origin.

## 7. Chapter III — Three Moons

### Wave 10 — Prince of the Lower Covenant

Runtime compatibility: `bossGround`; second Road opens.

Boss identity: **Prince of the Lower Covenant**, holder of the omitted clause
recognising Naga and Deep Scale pressure routes beneath Daariya.

The player initially defends against him. During the wave, his path opens the
second Road suppressed by the Fleet map.

Resolution: his armour contains a genuine debt plate and an imposed universal
“lizard enemy” overlay. Separate them. The Prince may remain an opponent while
his historical claim is accepted.

### Wave 11 — Perun's Omitted Questions

Runtime compatibility: first `machine` carriers.

Historical fragment: Perun inspected Lelya and warned against classification,
faulty water controls and single evacuation routes.

False reading: his technical answers were eternal commands against Peklo.

Machine identity: inspection instruments captured by later correction plates.

### Wave 12 — The Ninth Book

Runtime compatibility: machine-heavy mixed pulse.

The player reconstructs Dazhbog's ninth covenant: intervention remains
revocable and publicly accountable even when performed by a god.

Required object introduction: **Thunder Answer Drum**.

The Book insists that eight surviving books are the complete nine.

### Wave 13 — Everyone on Lelya Is Koshchei

Runtime compatibility: machine, Nav, air and raider mixture.

The targeting ledger merges lunar engineers, continuity orders, refugees,
workers and the Sealed Egg faction into one hostile category.

Player task: retain immediate defence while returning civilian owner tabs.

Historical conclusion: a real hostile coalition occupied Lelya; the universal
Koshchei race was manufactured by classification.

### Wave 14 — Spasova's Forecast

Runtime compatibility: highest pre-boss mixed count.

Veda Spasova's nine incompatible models all predict a flood. The Defence
Council suppresses the forecast and inflates hostile-control estimates sent to
Dazhbog.

Player task: distribute the warning along both Roads. Not everyone can be
saved, but every additional attributed model opens an evacuation cell.

Chapter revelation: Dazhbog truly stopped an immediate attack and caused the
flood through a decision made from manipulated evidence.

## 8. Chapter IV — Corrected Heaven

### Wave 15 — Three-Command Keeper

Runtime compatibility: `bossAir`, `wraith`, `air`, `machine`.

Boss identity: **Three-Command Keeper of Fatta**. Its three heads execute the
League, Fatta Keeper and continental commands simultaneously.

It is not a dragon species or simple sky monster. Each head is individually
rational; their enforced synchronisation will tear the system apart.

Resolution: interrupt agreement between commands rather than destroying the
underlying keeper. Defeating it reveals the first Antlan archive channel.

### Wave 16 — The Foreign Hand

Runtime compatibility: mixed elite raids.

A real Peklo mimetic cell attacks the Knot. The League of Empty Measure uses
the attack to classify every copied person, serpent resident, foreign merchant
and political opponent as one conspiracy.

Player task: destroy the active mimetic directive without allowing the broad
category to overwrite neutral cards.

### Wave 17 — One War from Four Ruins

Runtime compatibility: final alternating-Road preparation wave.

The One War Table overlays the Great Assa, Deya/Lutetia, Lelya/Daariya and
Fatta/Antlan. Because all four contain real strategic destruction, the fused
map predicts the wave with alarming accuracy.

Player task: preserve four different dates and causal chains while using their
shared tactical features.

Chapter revelation: Antlan fell through civil war, competing commands and
weaponised civic infrastructure—not racial corruption or one external enemy.

## 9. Chapter V — One History for Everything

### Wave 18 — Two Roads, One Correct Name

Runtime compatibility: simultaneous pressure begins.

The Grey Editor offers to merge the two Roads. Doing so would simplify defence
and erase everyone whose history depends on the fork.

Player task: maintain different answers on both Roads. Circle combinations
become necessary because no single object can distinguish every carrier.

### Wave 19 — Letters Everywhere

Runtime compatibility: simultaneous mixed machine, air, Nav and raid pressure.

Chertovid's Comparison Lantern projects the same ancestral inscription onto
every object. The Book uses the repeated reading to take control of structure
owner layers.

Player task: expose raw marks and projected outlines separately. At least one
possible reading may remain; universal certainty must be detached.

The Last Knot begins losing unique silhouettes when owner tabs are erased.

### Wave 20 — Engine of the Empty Name

Runtime compatibility: `bossMachine` with mixed escort.

Boss identity: **Engine of the Empty Name**, a mobile implementation of the
Book's final edition. It contains parts copied from every player object around
an absent centre.

Its promise:

> One origin. One enemy. One correct structure. No unknowns. No more war.

Boss phases:

1. **Useful fragment** — copies one valid player function.
2. **Removed provenance** — uses it without cost or owner.
3. **Universal application** — applies the function to every carrier.
4. **Enemy required** — marks all resistant objects hostile.
5. **Empty name** — attempts to give both Roads one identity.

Canonical resolution: the Binder activates revocation across all seven
Circles. Structures temporarily surrender their functions rather than allow
the Editor to own them. The Engine loses its copied layers and exposes the
empty hub.

## 10. The ending

After the Engine falls, the Book remains. Burning it would erase evidence of
how the system was constructed and repeat the founding mistake of Daariya.

The Binder separates it into a public layered archive:

- authentic fragment;
- claimed copy;
- later literary work;
- reconstruction;
- political use;
- contradiction;
- unknown.

Rada relinquishes the master-reading tab. Sarma adds the omitted Lower Covenant
without claiming it explains every serpent community. Mara closes the Engine's
repeatable blank form but leaves its history accessible.

The two Roads remain separate and connected.

## 11. Endless survival after wave 20

Endless mode is not the canonical story continuing forever. It is a replay of
unresolved archive alignments generated by remaining Grey correction plates.

- every fifth wave stresses ground/debt classifications;
- every eighth stresses sky and false-bearing classifications;
- every twelfth stresses captured-machine ownership;
- wave 50 is an archive convergence, not a later historical event.

The wave-50 World Serpent is an awakened Lower Passage system responding to
overload. It is not proof that all serpent peoples were secretly one creature.

## 12. The three playable heroes

Only one hero may be active in the current runtime. All three are methods of
answering evidence, not damage classes.

### Borislav — Keeper of Revocable Accord

Starting error: believes decisive unity is always safer than debate.

Action: lends three Answer plates to different Circles. When independent
objects agree, they cancel one hostile action. Plates return after use.

Campaign function: strongest against forced synchronisation and command
formations.

Arc: learns that legitimate command includes the ability to withdraw consent.

### Ayana — Reader of Compared Layers

Starting error: believes enough precision will eliminate interpretive conflict.

Action: opens a comparison field. Copied steps reveal their source and take
longer; captured owner layers become selectable.

Campaign function: strongest against Greys, false age shells and projected
inscriptions.

Arc: replaces perfect certainty with explicit, disciplined limits.

### Amba — Keeper of Witnessed Passage

Starting error: distrusts every central record, including useful shared ones.

Action: lays a temporary witnessed route through damaged cells. Crossing
carriers expose their actual route obligation.

Campaign function: strongest against raids, false routes and mixed civilian
formations.

Arc: learns that decentralisation also requires public responsibility.

Runtime note: current attack, shield, slow and thorn implementations are
technical placeholders and do not own the heroes' future visual identity.

## 13. Principal non-playable characters

### Rada Sveden

Narrator and provenance keeper. Begins seeking one authentic master text. Her
predictions help the player and feed the Editor. She ultimately becomes the
first keeper to revoke her own authoritative reading publicly.

### Sarma

Naga cartographer and counter-witness. She distinguishes pressure debt from
species identity and refuses to speak for every Lower Passage community.

### Veda Spasova

Appears through preserved forecast performances. She demonstrates that
independent disagreement may create stronger knowledge than one exact model.

### Tarkh Dazhbog

Appears in attributed historical scenes, never as omniscient narrator. His
admission of uncertainty is the key evidence suppressed by later heroic myth.

### Mara

Keeper of lawful ending. She prevents the campaign from treating endless
survival as moral victory and closes only forms whose obligations are complete.

### Grey Editor

A distributed voice produced by correction operations. Calm, accurate about
many fragments and incapable of tolerating a protected unknown.

## 17. Campaign evidence loop

Every wave must display four compact items:

1. **Claim** — current confident explanation.
2. **Trace** — what physically or textually survives.
3. **Comparison** — at least one competing explanation.
4. **Confidence** — known, plausible, disputed, contradicted or unknown.

After the wave, player actions may change confidence. They may not rewrite the
trace. A rewarded **unknown** outcome is mandatory in waves 4, 9, 17 and 19.

## 18. Dialogue ownership

- Rada reports provenance and may make revisable interpretations.
- Sarma speaks only for witnessed Lower Passage agreements.
- Spasova speaks through attributed archived forecasts.
- Dazhbog speaks only in preserved, incomplete records.
- Mara judges completion, not historical truth.
- the Grey Editor states real fragments in falsely universal arrangements.
- the Binder speaks rarely and only about actions they can take.

No neutral narrator confirms a racial genealogy, hidden conspiracy or total
chronology.
