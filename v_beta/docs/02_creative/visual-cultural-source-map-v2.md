# Visual Cultural Source Map v2

Status: canonical research and adaptation policy  
Updated: 2026-07-23

## Purpose

This document controls how cultural references become original game design.
References provide material, construction and compositional principles. They are
not decal libraries.

## Ancient Rus and East Slavic folklore

### Use

- timber forts, log construction, plank roofs, river craft, smithing and rope;
- birch-bark documents and containers;
- manuscript initials, carved boards, seals and inventory marks;
- Perun, Veles, Mokosh, Dazhbog and Stribog as named fictional powers;
- domovoi, leshy, rusalki, zmey, bogatyr, Baba Yaga, Koschei, Sirin and Alkonost
  as broad cultural archetypes.

### Do not use

- random pseudo-runes;
- one modern reconstruction presented as uncontested antiquity;
- copied museum ornament without attribution;
- exact film, animation or illustration character designs.

## Soviet layer

### Use

- expedition cases, archive folders, technical diagrams and map symbols;
- enamel equipment panels, numbered modules, warning stripes and toggle guards;
- radio cabinets, optical instruments, geodesic structures, coils and cables;
- monumental sun, star, orbit and knowledge compositions as broad geometry;
- practical repair and reuse rather than polished retrofuturism.

### Do not use

- exact state emblems as the universal player faction logo;
- exact military hardware or identifiable weapons where an original machine
  serves the gameplay better;
- nostalgia advertising or clean theme-park “USSR aesthetic”.

## Post-Soviet layer

### Use

- patched roofing, mismatched replacement panels, handwritten corrections;
- faded paint, oxidized fasteners, reused containers and improvised power;
- the coexistence of obsolete equipment with new cables and screens.

### Do not use

- misery as decoration;
- current political insignia;
- direct references to ongoing real conflicts.

## Russian Far East

### Environment

Sopki, river valleys, taiga, cedar and pine, fog, wet stone, fish, water birds,
bears, seasonal ice, ports, geological camps and long infrastructure distances.

### Indigenous cultural research

Nanai, Ulchi, Udege, Nivkh, Evenki, Oroch, Negidal, Uilta and Ainu sources must
be researched and named separately.

Allowed high-level principles include:

- birch bark, wood, fish skin and textile as distinct material languages;
- central rosette plus border composition;
- nested motifs;
- plant, fish, bird, bear and serpent forms;
- asymmetry inside a balanced whole.

Restricted use:

- ritual clothing, shamanic objects, clan signs and sacred figures require a
  specific source note and a reason tied to a specific character;
- no pan-ethnic “shaman ornament”;
- no copying living ceremonial designs as generic tower trim.

## Original symbol system

The project creates its own readable marks:

- Zastava: open circle with three short layer notches;
- Grey: closed pale lens bisected by a thin vertical slit;
- Lower City: broken spiral scale with an outward tooth;
- damaged Nav: incomplete double contour and missing center;
- Kitezh-17: rectangular archive frame crossed by a circular site coordinate.

These symbols must be drawn specifically for the game and checked against common
political, extremist, military and commercial marks before release.

## Recognizable archetypes

The following may inspire object roles without copying a protected depiction:

- Koschei: externalized death and impossible containment;
- Baba Yaga: moving dwelling, mortar, boundary guide;
- Zmey Gorynych: multi-headed aerial threat;
- bogatyr: defender with iconic equipment;
- Sirin/Alkonost: prophetic bird imagery;
- leshy: living path and forest misdirection;
- Soviet scientist, geologist, radio operator and cosmonaut as professions rather
  than portraits of identifiable people.

## Cardboard translation

Every source becomes:

1. a large silhouette;
2. a small set of separately cut modules;
3. matte printed color over visible paper fiber;
4. a compact contact shadow between layers;
5. sparse original ornament on no more than one or two secondary pieces.

The result must read first as an Ether Frontier game object, second as a cultural
reference, and never as a museum object copied onto a token.
