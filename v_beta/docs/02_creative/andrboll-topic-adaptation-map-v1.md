# Ether Frontier — Andrboll Topic Adaptation Map v1

Status: **canonical thematic backbone for world and campaign**  
Updated: 2026-07-26

Detailed reconstruction of the cosmic pseudo-history is maintained in
`ingliist-cosmic-myth-research-v1.md`. It is research input, not historical
fact and not direct game canon.

## 1. Direction correction

The campaign must not be a generic story about misinformation. It must examine
the same connected subject field as Andrboll's documentary “Я ведаю, так что и
вам придётся”: alternative etymology, invented antiquity, the “Veles Book”,
reconstruction of Slavic paganism, post-Soviet neo-pagan authors, Ingliism,
racial and cosmic mythology, pareidolic epigraphy, Tartaria and buried-city
catastrophism.

The game uses the same investigative movement:

1. let a claim speak in its appealing complete form;
2. identify its immediate author and source;
3. follow where that author borrowed from;
4. compare the claim with surviving texts, linguistics, archaeology and the
   history of publication;
5. show how the claim changes in the next retelling;
6. follow the transition from entertainment to doctrine, commerce, identity
   and political radicalisation;
7. make the contradiction itself produce satire.

The game does not copy Andrboll's narration, wording, montage or jokes. It
adapts the investigated themes and the source-tracing method.

## 2. The world metaphor

The Last Knot receives **Books of Direction** from different periods. Each Book
claims to explain where the Roads came from. Most contain some authentic
fragment, but every new compiler adds pages, diagrams and enemies required to
make the previous version complete.

The campaign follows one travelling manuscript called the **Book That
Remembered Too Much**. It begins as a collection of entertaining word
similarities. By the final act it contains ancient starships, erased moons,
hidden races, universal architecture and an enemy responsible for every gap.

The manuscript is not a magic evil object. Different people repeatedly rewrite
it because it gives them wonder, belonging, authority, income or a simple
explanation of loss.

## 3. Nine thematic arcs

## Arc I — The Sunny Root

### Real subject focus

- popular alternative etymology;
- decomposition of words into invented meaningful roots;
- the claim that European words descend from Russian;
- “Ra” and similar universal word keys;
- claims about pre-Christian writing based on ambiguous passages.

### World transformation

The travelling performer **Zvonar Radomir** discovers that several Road names
contain the same bright syllable. His joyful stage game becomes the **Sunny
Root Table**, which can derive any word from any other word by rotating pieces.

### Satirical escalation

The first demonstrations are genuinely funny and memorable. Trouble begins
when road signs are physically rerouted because their names “prove” where they
must lead.

### Gameplay

Enemies split name cards into convenient fragments and attach identical root
tabs. The player compares sound history, written form and date. Similarity may
remain; universal descent must be detached.

## Arc II — The Cart of the First People

### Real subject focus

- Proto-Indo-European reconstruction;
- the Kurgan hypothesis;
- Indo-Iranian use of “Aryan”;
- modern Slavs incorrectly identified with all ancient Indo-Europeans;
- language, archaeological culture and Y-chromosome haplogroup collapsed into
  one biological nation;
- Sanskrit and Lithuanian similarities used as proof of Russian primacy.

### World transformation

The **Cartographers of the First Cart** overlay three different maps: speech,
burial practice and blood lineage. Their transparent layers partially match,
so they rivet them into one map and declare a single travelling people.

### Satirical escalation

Every new migration arrow points back to the cartographers' own village.

### Gameplay

The player must keep language cards, material-culture cards and ancestry cards
on separate pivots. Combining them is allowed only locally and provisionally.

## Arc III — The Missing Boards

### Real subject focus

- the “Veles Book” as a central primary object of the movement;
- the Isenbek boards and Mirolyubov transmission story;
- disappearance of originals;
- late publication and unstable versions;
- linguistic anachronisms and artificial pseudo-archaic language;
- editions associated with Alexander Asov.

### World transformation

The antiquarian **Mirodar** claims to have copied sacred wooden boards before
they vanished in a fire, flood, evacuation and customs inspection, in that
order. Only his paper copies remain, and every edition restores more text than
the previous edition possessed.

### Satirical escalation

Missing boards become stronger evidence than surviving boards because no
skeptic can inspect them.

### Gameplay

Copies spawn artificial age layers. Root tests continuity, Name tracks edition
history, and Sun exposes modern repairs. The player cannot destroy the book;
they must keep “late literary work”, “claimed copy” and “ancient source” as
different states.

## Arc IV — The Pantheon with Empty Sockets

### Real subject focus

- limited and fragmentary evidence for pre-Christian Slavic religion;
- chronicles, archaeology and comparative interpretation;
- reconstructions associated with Boris Rybakov;
- Perun, Veles, Dazhbog, Mokosh, Svarog, Khors, Stribog, Simargl, Rod and Mother
  Damp Earth;
- later systems presented with far more certainty and detail than sources
  allow.

### World transformation

The **Museum of Eleven Empty Sockets** displays a few surviving figures and
many blank mounts. Successive keepers dislike empty space and commission a new
god for every unused socket, then a family tree explaining all commissions.

### Satirical escalation

The museum guide becomes longer than the evidence hall.

### Gameplay

Some sockets must remain unknown. Filling every socket gives short-term power
but allows the Grey Editor to standardise the pantheon.

## Arc V — The Bonfire of Restoration

### Real subject focus

- early Russian Rodnoverie;
- Dobroslav/Alexei Dobrovolsky;
- reconstruction mixed with political ideology;
- radical nationalism, symbolism and the transition from cultural revival to
  exclusionary doctrine.

### World transformation

The former dissident **Dobrozhig** begins by restoring seasonal communal fires
and local ecological practice. He later declares that only one kind of person
may stand inside the fire circle and that every borrowed custom is betrayal.

### Satirical escalation

The ritual intended to restore community becomes an entrance checkpoint.

### Gameplay

The player preserves ecological and communal practices while removing owner
tests, purity gates and hostile category cards. The satire must show why the
early restoration was attractive before showing its radicalisation.

## Arc VI — The Academy of Several Moons

### Real subject focus

- Alexander Khinevich and Ingliism;
- “Slavic-Aryan Vedas” and claims of transmitted ancient texts;
- invented arithmetic and writing systems;
- Asgard of Iriy, vaytmanas, several moons and cosmic migration;
- “Grey” aliens and racial cosmology;
- institutional appearance: church, school, lectures and printed books.

### World transformation

The **Rector of the Irian Academy** receives sealed books from unnamed elders.
His school teaches an arithmetic in which contradictory results prove that the
student used an enemy calendar. The ceiling gains extra moons whenever a new
edition needs one.

### Satirical escalation

Local folklore becomes an interplanetary administrative history complete with
vehicle regulations, lunar demolitions and ancestral immigration forms.

### Gameplay

Players dismantle the chain `anonymous elders → sealed book → school diagram →
physical moon`. They must distinguish imaginative cosmology from claims about
real peoples. Grey enemies appear here first as a story category invented by
the Academy, not as a literal race.

## Arc VII — The Falcon Lecturer

### Real subject focus

- Alexei Trekhlebov as populariser and continuer of Ingliist ideas;
- lectures about races, peoples, “Greys” and mixture;
- movement of doctrine from closed institutional texts into charismatic video
  performance and personal instruction;
- “Кощуны Финиста Ясного Сокола России”.

### World transformation

The travelling lecturer **Trekhleb** carries simplified Academy diagrams from
Road to Road. Each retelling removes a footnote and adds a rule for classifying
the audience.

### Satirical escalation

The doctrine becomes easier to remember precisely as it becomes harsher and
less qualified.

### Gameplay

Wind traces copied lecture routes. Name restores removed footnotes. Audience
cards begin as listeners and become colour-coded categories unless protected.

## Arc VIII — Letters Everywhere

### Real subject focus

- Valery Chudinov;
- alleged hidden inscriptions on stones, images, artefacts and planets;
- visual enhancement and selective tracing;
- pareidolia;
- self-confirming interpretation in which every surface repeats the expected
  words.

### World transformation

The reader **Chertovid** owns a Comparison Lantern that can find the same name
in bark, clouds, masonry and the face of the moon. Each discovered letter is a
real detachable shape; only the grouping is imposed.

### Satirical escalation

Once every object contains the same inscription, the inscription no longer
distinguishes anything — which Chertovid treats as proof of universality.

### Gameplay

Sun reveals raw marks and projected outlines as separate layers. Players may
keep a possible reading, but must prevent the outline from burning itself into
the object.

## Arc IX — What Buried the Cities?

### Real subject focus

- Great Tartaria narratives;
- similar imperial/classical architecture treated as evidence of one global
  civilisation;
- buried lower floors;
- catastrophe or war in the nineteenth century;
- ordinary urban rebuilding, soil accumulation and reused structures rejected
  as insufficiently dramatic;
- national histories presented as deliberate theft or renaming.

### World transformation

The **Society of the Lower Window** excavates every cellar and joins photographs
of unrelated cities into one continuous street. Because every street has a
lower window, they conclude that the world was buried on the same afternoon.

### Satirical escalation

Maps are redrawn to make architectural style sovereign territory. Buildings
that do not fit receive artificial buried floors overnight.

### Gameplay

Stone preserves stratigraphy; Water tracks flooding and soil; Name keeps
construction and renovation dates. The final boss is not a Tartarian emperor
but a self-expanding **Universal Before Photograph** that replaces local
histories with one panorama.

## 4. Campaign grouping

The nine arcs occupy twenty missions:

| Missions | Arc |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Sunny Root |
| 3–4 | First Cart |
| 5–7 | Missing Boards |
| 8–9 | Empty-Socket Pantheon |
| 10–11 | Bonfire of Restoration |
| 12–14 | Academy of Several Moons |
| 15–16 | Falcon Lecturer |
| 17–18 | Letters Everywhere |
| 19–20 | Buried Cities |

The same claim evolves between arcs. Wordplay supplies ancient vocabulary;
the First Cart supplies ancestors; Missing Boards supply scripture; the Museum
supplies gods; the Bonfire supplies identity discipline; the Academy supplies
cosmos and enemies; the Lecturer distributes it; Chertovid finds proof on
every surface; the Society supplies a physically erased global civilisation.

## 5. Satirical tone

The narrator is curious, dry and willing to be corrected. They never say “only
an idiot believes this”. Instead, objects visibly obey a claim until its hidden
assumption produces an absurd consequence.

Satire alternates:

- sincere wonder at language, archaeology and mythology;
- exact reconstruction of the claim;
- a small verification question;
- escalating practical consequences;
- a human reason someone wants to keep believing;
- a clear boundary when the idea becomes racist, exclusionary or conspiratorial.

## 6. Research boundary

Real authors and publications remain named in the research matrix. Fictional
characters are composites and may not reproduce a living or historical
person's appearance, voice, catchphrases or biography one-to-one.

Scientific and academic sources do not become an infallible “good faction”.
They provide methods, evidence and limits. The player must be able to mark an
academic reconstruction as disputed or outdated too.
