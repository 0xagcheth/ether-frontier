# Ether Frontier — Ingliist Cosmic Myth Research v1

Status: **research reconstruction; not historical fact and not direct canon**  
Updated: 2026-07-26

## 1. Scope and warning

This document reconstructs the internal pseudo-history associated with
Alexander Khinevich's Ingliism, the “Slavic-Aryan Vedas”, later popularisers
and adjacent alternative-history retellings discussed in Andrboll's film.

It does not treat the material as ancient history. Several source texts contain
racial hierarchy, prohibitions on intermarriage and antisemitic coding. Those
elements are documented here because they are structurally central to the myth
and to its satirical critique; they must not be repeated as valid world facts or
used to target real groups in the game.

The corpus is unstable. Dates, names and causal chains differ between editions,
lectures, derivative websites and later authors. “Variant” is therefore an
important research result, not a defect to conceal.

## 2. Cosmogenesis

### Ra-M-Ha and Ingliya

In the Ingliist system, the inaccessible first principle **Ra-M-Ha** manifests
the primordial living fire or light called **Ingliya**. From it arise worlds,
gods and living beings. The invented etymology of names is itself part of the
cosmology: syllables are treated as stable packets of cosmic meaning.

The result combines:

- a remote absolute principle;
- a sacred generative fire;
- genealogies of gods treated as literal ancestors;
- a technological universe of inhabited “Earths” orbiting many suns;
- moral geography divided into Light and Dark worlds.

### Golden Path and hierarchy of worlds

The universe contains many-dimensional worlds joined by the **Golden Path of
spiritual ascent**. Commonly named levels include:

- **Yav** — manifested material worlds;
- **Nav** — non-material or post-mortal worlds, sometimes subdivided;
- **Slav** — worlds of luminous ancestors and gods;
- **Prav** — higher ordering or divine law;
- **Peklo** — a group of Dark worlds and, in some explanations, a hostile
  galaxy or region rather than merely an underworld;
- **Interworld/Borderland** — transition spaces through which ships and beings
  move.

The system freely combines spiritual planes, galaxies, dimensions and moral
categories. Later lecturers often present them as one navigable cosmographic
map.

### Mara

“World of Mara” is not stable across the derivative corpus. It can mean:

- a cold or death-associated domain under the power of Mara/Marena;
- a region of Nav connected with cessation and winter;
- a Dark-world designation in popular retellings;
- an astronomical association: Fatta is said in some cosmograms to “pulse in
  the Hall of Marena”.

For adaptation, Mara should remain a boundary of ended forms, while characters
inside the myth incorrectly collapse death, cold, enemy territory and cosmic
coordinates into one literal place.

## 3. Gods as cosmic relatives and administrators

The “gods” are not consistently transcendent creators. They frequently behave
as powerful ancestors, travellers, military commanders, teachers and guardians
of star systems.

### Perun

Perun, son of Svarog, arrives at Asgard Iriysky in a **Vaitmana**, described as
a celestial chariot or spacecraft. “Santias of the Vedas of Perun” are framed
as his dialogue with Slavs and Aryans roughly forty thousand years ago. He
warns of coming Dark times and hostile beings from Peklo.

### Tarkh Dazhbog

Tarkh/Dazhbog becomes a cosmic protector rather than only a historically
attested deity. He:

- gives or guards sacred wisdom;
- is connected with the identity “grandchildren of Dazhbog”;
- destroys the moon **Lelya**, occupied by hostile “Koshchei”;
- thereby saves Midgard in the short term but causes a planetary flood when
  fragments fall;
- is retroactively linked to the custom of striking painted eggs: the broken
  egg becomes the “egg of Koshchei”, the intact one the force of Tarkh.

This is a key satirical mechanism: a familiar seasonal custom is converted into
memory of an orbital military operation through wordplay and visual analogy.

### Svarog, Chernobog and the Great Assa

Svarog governs luminous order and life. Chernobog breaks seals protecting
ancient wisdom, allowing knowledge or beings from Dark worlds to enter the
ascending path. The **Great Assa** is an immense conflict between forces of
Light and Dark across multiple worlds.

The mythology treats theology, racial genealogy, galactic war and moral law as
different views of one administrative system.

## 4. The Great Race

### Four lineages

“Great Race” is explained as “Clans of the Aesir from the Land of the Aesir”
and divided into four supposedly white cosmic lineages:

1. **Da'Aryans**;
2. **Kh'Aryans**;
3. **Rasens**;
4. **Svyatorus**.

Derivative sources assign them different eye colours, star systems, abilities,
scripts and modern descendants. These assignments vary and should not be
treated as one stable canon even within the movement.

The four groups supposedly arrive from different inhabited systems but already
share language, ancestry, religion and social order. This circularly makes
their unity both the premise and the conclusion.

### Race as cosmic moral category

The word “race” is used biologically, spiritually, culturally and politically
at once. Whiteness becomes evidence of divine ancestry; mixture is framed as
degradation; modern peoples are classified by supposed cosmic descent.

This is not optional background. It is the point where space opera becomes a
racial ideology. A satirical adaptation must expose the classification machine
and never simply replace the names while retaining its hierarchy.

## 5. Arrival on Midgard-Earth

### Vaitmaras and Vaitmanas

The ancestors travel in large interworld ships often called **Vaitmaras**, with
smaller **Vaitmanas** used as shuttles. Later popular culture freely equates
these with Indian vimanas and modern spacecraft.

### Daariya

The first great settlement is **Daariya**, placed at the North Pole and equated
with Hyperborea, Arctida or other northern lost lands. It is divided by four
rivers into territories associated with the four Great Race lineages.

Daariya functions as:

- first homeland on Midgard;
- proof of polar antiquity;
- origin point for later migration;
- explanation for northern myths and maps;
- source of “ancestral memory”.

### Great Migration

Before the destruction of Lelya, a priest named **Spas** foresees the flood.
The Great Race evacuates from Daariya along the Riphean Mountains, identified
with the Urals, toward Siberia and other lands. The evacuation is sometimes
connected with the invented holiday etymology **PASKHET** — “Path by which the
Aesir walked”.

### Asgard Iriysky

Asgard Iriysky is located at the confluence of the rivers Iriy and Om, mapped
onto modern Irtysh and Om near Omsk. One popular chronology dates its foundation
to 104,778 BCE during a conjunction of three moons. Later retellings claim it
survived until 1530 CE and was destroyed by Dzungars, creating a bridge from
cosmic antiquity directly into documented early-modern Siberia.

## 6. Midgard's three moons

The most common arrangement is:

| Moon | Claimed period | Narrative role |
|---|---:|---|
| Lelya | 7 days | small nearest moon; occupied by hostile Koshchei and destroyed by Tarkh Dazhbog |
| Fatta | 13 days | transferred from Deya; later linked to Antlan/Atlantis catastrophe |
| Mesyats | 29.5 days | surviving present moon |

The periods are then used to “explain” cultural numbers, calendars and words.
For example, Fatta is connected to “fatality” and the unlucky number thirteen.

## 7. First lunar catastrophe: Lelya

### Hostile occupation

“Foreigners” or Koshchei occupy Lelya and allegedly prepare to destroy or
control Midgard. In several summaries these beings are connected with Dark
worlds or the “Greys”.

### Destruction

Tarkh Dazhbog destroys Lelya with a directed weapon. Spas has warned that its
fragments will strike Midgard.

### Consequences

- fragments fall to Earth;
- the planetary axis shifts;
- continents move;
- a global flood destroys Daariya;
- survivors disperse into Rassenia and other lands;
- flood myths and spring customs become distorted memories of the event.

Popular chronologies place this around 111–113 thousand years ago, but exact
numbers vary between the event, evacuation and commemorative calendar.

## 8. Deya, Lutetia and the asteroid belt

A separate cosmic-war cycle concerns the planet **Deya** and its satellite
**Lutetia/Lucifer**. Versions differ:

- Dark forces destroy a moon or planet and create the asteroid belt;
- luminous forces move Deya through Interworld and transfer Fatta to Midgard;
- the explosion damages the atmosphere of **Arey** (Mars);
- survivors from damaged worlds are resettled on Midgard;
- some inhabitants of Mars retreat underground.

This cycle absorbs the asteroid belt, Mars's thin atmosphere and imagined
underground Martians into the same war chronicle.

## 9. Ants and Antlan

### Ants

The Ants are placed inside the Great Race genealogy but become a politically
separate civilisation. Their country **Antlan** is equated with Atlantis on a
large Atlantic island.

### Rebellion and technological war

In one developed derivative version, Antlan's priests and princes are corrupted
or persuaded by demons. They attempt to change the orbit of **Net-Miru/Nibiru**
and use:

- gravitational weapons;
- tectonic weapons;
- climate weapons;
- nuclear weapons.

This is presented as a civil war inside the supposedly unified Slavic-Aryan
world, which complicates the simple Light Race versus external enemy story.

### Fatta catastrophe

Orbital experiments and war destroy or destabilise Fatta. Its fragments strike
Midgard; Antlan sinks; climate and continents change. This catastrophe is
commonly placed about thirteen thousand years ago.

The corpus often blends:

- the fall of Fatta;
- the sinking of Atlantis/Antlan;
- the end of an ice-age civilisation;
- a nuclear war;
- myths of global flood;
- later stories of buried cities.

## 10. Greys

### Internal role

“Greys” or grey-skinned foreigners are presented as beings from Dark worlds or
the galaxy/region Peklo. They are said to be androgynous or dual-sexed, unable
to create in the same way as the Great Race, and dependent on deception,
mixture or appropriation.

### Ideological function

The image merges UFO folklore with antisemitic conspiracy coding. Some texts
explicitly turn the Greys into ancestors or controllers of real human groups.
This material was among the reasons the published corpus was found to contain
racial superiority and anti-miscegenation content.

### Adaptation rule

The game may preserve “Greys” as a fictional cosmic bureaucracy only if it
breaks every mapping to real ethnicity, blood or religion. The satire should
show an author constructing an enemy category because the theory requires one.

## 11. Lizards, Nagas and underground peoples

“Lizards” are less stable than the Greys in the core corpus. Popular retellings
combine several motifs:

- Nagas from Indian mythology;
- Dravidians and Nagas classified as non-white opponents or pupils;
- underground serpent peoples;
- reptilian conspiracy folklore;
- surviving inhabitants of damaged planets living below ground;
- Koshchei or Dark-world servants reimagined as reptilians.

The result is not one canonical species. Andrboll-style treatment should show
how later lecturers fuse separate Indian, UFO, racial and fantasy motifs into a
single “lizard enemy”.

For the game, distinct Lower-Serpent communities are preferable: some enemies,
some allies, none a racial analogue. Their diversity can satirise the source
myth's urge to collapse every serpent image into one conspiracy.

## 12. Claimed nuclear destruction of civilisation

There is no single war event across all versions. At least four destruction
cycles are often merged:

1. the Great Assa across Light and Dark worlds;
2. destruction around Deya/Lutetia and damage to Mars;
3. destruction of Lelya and the flood of Daariya;
4. Antlan's civil war, Fatta's fall and a thirteen-thousand-year catastrophe.

Later Tartarian narratives may add:

5. a much more recent war or catastrophe in the eighteenth or nineteenth
   century, invoked to explain deserts, melted buildings and buried floors.

“Nuclear war destroyed the ancient civilisation” is therefore a compression of
several incompatible timelines. This instability is ideal for satire: different
ministries can issue mutually exclusive commemorative calendars for the same
ruin.

## 13. How the system absorbs evidence

The mythology grows through recurring operations:

- **name equivalence:** similar sounds prove identity (`Antlan` → Atlantis,
  `Iriy` → Irtysh, `Asgard` → city of Aesir);
- **numerical backfill:** lunar periods explain unlucky numbers and calendars;
- **ritual backfill:** painted eggs preserve a destroyed hostile moon;
- **geographic backfill:** Omsk overlays Asgard; the North Pole overlays
  Hyperborea/Daariya;
- **myth merger:** Indian vimanas, Norse Asgard, Slavic gods and UFO lore become
  one technological history;
- **absence as proof:** missing plates, cities or records show deliberate
  destruction;
- **scale escalation:** a local linguistic or ritual resemblance expands into
  planetary administration.

## 14. Contradictions worth preserving in satire

- The Great Race is perfectly unified but repeatedly fights civil wars.
- Gods are spiritual ancestors, biological relatives, star travellers and
  military officers depending on the scene.
- Dark beings are technologically superior yet described as incapable of
  independent creation.
- The same flood is attributed to different moons and wars.
- The same cultural custom proves several unrelated cosmic events.
- Every modern place name is ancient evidence, while inconvenient historical
  names are later falsifications.
- A secret exterminated civilisation leaves evidence in universally familiar
  holidays, words, city plans and lecture diagrams.
- Exact dates coexist with no inspectable primary artefact.

## 15. Recommended game-world extraction

Preserve as fictional raw material:

- Midgard as one inhabited Earth among many;
- Vaitmaras and Vaitmanas;
- Daariya and the evacuation;
- Asgard Iriysky under three moons;
- Tarkh Dazhbog's destruction of Lelya;
- Antlan's civil war and Fatta catastrophe;
- Mars/Arey survivors and underground routes;
- several competing calendars of destruction;
- Greys as an invented enemy category that becomes material;
- multiple unrelated serpent communities collapsed by propaganda;
- ruins whose official plaques contradict one another.

Transform or reject:

- white racial supremacy;
- real-world ethnic mappings;
- anti-miscegenation doctrine;
- antisemitic coding of Greys;
- biological hierarchy of the four lineages;
- claims of historical authenticity.
