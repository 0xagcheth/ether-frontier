# Ether Frontier — First Cosmic Age World Bible v1

Status: **canonical first-age world layer**  
Updated: 2026-07-26

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the first cosmic age of Ether Frontier: the origin of
the worlds, the Great Assa, the four lineages of the Great Race, the Greys, the
serpent peoples, Tarkh Dazhbog, Perun and the arrival on Midgard.

It adapts the internal mythology examined in Andrboll's documentary and the
Ingliist pseudo-historical corpus. It is satire, not a statement about human
history, ethnicity or religion.

The world must initially feel magnificent and internally persuasive. Its
contradictions become visible through administration, competing chronicles and
material consequences. Characters do not wink at the audience.

## 2. The beginning according to the First Edition

Before space, time and measurement there was **Ra-M-Ha**, about whom the First
Edition states only:

> Ra-M-Ha was before the first difference.

When Ra-M-Ha became aware of its own existence, the awareness produced the
living fire **Ingliya**. Ingliya did not illuminate an existing universe. Its
movement created the distinction between inside and outside, before and after,
source and reflection.

From Ingliya arose innumerable worlds. No surviving witness observed this.
Every later civilisation nevertheless keeps an exact diagram.

### Satirical contradiction

The oldest cosmological plate contains one line and no illustration. The
official explanatory appendix contains 6,412 pages, fourteen ministries and
three mutually exclusive maps of the moment before geometry.

## 3. The structure of existence

The worlds are connected by the **Golden Path**, a network of passages through
which matter, memory and authority move. The same word “world” may describe a
planet, dimension, moral condition, government district or state of the dead.
Different institutions insist these meanings have always been identical.

### Yav — the manifested worlds

Yav contains matter, bodies, machines, weather and measurable time. Midgard is
one of its inhabited Earths.

Yav is not spiritually inferior. It is the only realm where a promise can be
tested against material consequences.

### Nav — the worlds of unfinished passage

Nav holds forms that have left one relationship but have not entered another:

- the dead whose names remain disputed;
- abandoned machines still following their last instruction;
- memories without owners;
- travellers whose destination world no longer exists;
- fragments detached during interworld transit.

Nav is not inherently evil. Its danger comes from assigning an expedient final
name to something still incomplete.

### Slav — the worlds of remembered ancestors

Slav contains ancestors, teachers and cultural forms preserved through active
remembrance. A being remains in Slav only while descendants may revise the
story told about it.

When revision is forbidden, the remembered ancestor hardens into an official
model and falls back toward Yav as a command.

### Prav — the worlds of binding order

Prav contains patterns that make relationships repeatable: measure, sequence,
reciprocity and revocation. Prav is not a list of moral commandments. It is the
condition under which an action can be attributed and reversed.

Later imperial theologians deliberately translate Prav as “the administration
is always correct”.

### Mara — the limit of completed forms

Mara is the domain in which forms are allowed to end. It is cold because no
new interpretation is permitted to consume a completed life. It is dark
because finished things no longer need to advertise themselves.

Mara is governed by no enemy race. **Mara the Keeper of the Last Shape** is the
office, person and law that protects endings from both resurrection and erasure.

The central dispute of the first age concerns Mara:

- one school calls it merciful completion;
- another calls it a prison of frozen potential;
- military chroniclers call it enemy territory;
- astronomers use the same name for the region through which Fatta once moved.

All four uses appear on official maps with the same border colour.

### Peklo — the compressed worlds

Peklo is a region where many worlds have been forced into too little room.
Energy, bodies and identities overlap. Its inhabitants developed survival
methods based on copying stable forms from outside.

Peklo is not hell. Calling it hell is a political translation made during the
Great Assa.

## 4. The gods

The word **god** has four legal meanings:

1. a being born in a higher-dimensional world;
2. an ancestor capable of interworld travel;
3. the holder of a cosmic office;
4. anyone whose vehicle arrives before the local dictionary can distinguish
   technology from divinity.

The ambiguity is never resolved.

### Svarog

Svarog maintains the great foundries where stable passages between worlds are
forged. He does not create matter from nothing; he makes different materials
agree on a shared boundary.

His followers later claim he personally designed every useful tool. Surviving
foundry invoices contradict this.

### Perun

Perun is Svarog's travelling inspector and commander of passage security. He
crosses the Golden Path in a small celestial vessel called a **Vaitmana**.

Perun's original task is to warn settlements when the path ahead passes near
compressed worlds. Later editions turn every inspection visit into a military
victory and every safety instruction into eternal scripture.

### Tarkh Dazhbog

Tarkh is the keeper of orbital agreements. **Dazhbog** is the title granted to
one who gives a settlement a sustainable relation with its sun.

He is:

- engineer of lunar routes;
- protector of inhabited Earths;
- distributor of nine books of navigation;
- ancestor by covenant of several travelling lineages;
- future destroyer of Lelya.

The phrase “grandchildren of Dazhbog” originally means communities operating
under his solar covenant. Later genealogists interpret it as literal biological
descent and begin issuing ancestry certificates.

### Chernobog

Chernobog is not the creator of evil. He argues that any sealed knowledge will
eventually become tyranny. He breaks the seals around the Archive of Arlegs and
allows fragments to fall into worlds unprepared to interpret them.

Some fragments save civilisations. Others allow village accountants to build
planet-destroying devices from incomplete maintenance diagrams.

Chernobog's act begins the Great Assa, although no chronicle agrees whether he
intended liberation, sabotage or a poorly supervised public-access programme.

## 5. The Great Assa

The **Great Assa** is not one war. It is the official name given to centuries
of migrations, rescue operations, civil conflicts and battles along the Golden
Path.

### Stated cause

The official Light Chronicle says the Dark worlds envied the creative power of
the luminous peoples and attempted to seize it.

### Surviving evidence

The surviving route records show:

- Peklo worlds were collapsing under compression;
- refugees copied foreign forms to survive transit;
- Light administrations restricted access to stable passages;
- both sides used copied identities;
- several battles began when rescue vessels were recorded as invasion fleets;
- Chernobog's released diagrams allowed small factions to build strategic
  weapons without understanding their safeguards.

### Satirical centre

Every faction calls itself the force of Light because “Light” is also the name
of the form used to request military funding.

## 6. The Great Race

The **Great Race** is the central civilisational identity of the luminous
coalition. Its official expansion is “the Clans of the Aesir from the Lands of
the Aesir”.

The term begins as a navigation category for vessels compatible with the same
life-support standard. It gradually becomes a civilisation, ancestry, moral
quality and alleged biological race.

## 7. The four lineages

The four lineages originate from different star systems. They are culturally
distinct but participate in the same migration fleet.

Their differences must be visible through craft, rhythm and institutional
practice—not through claims that one real human population descends from them.

### Da'Aryans — keepers of stable passage

Origin tradition: the system of Zimun near the northern guiding stars.

Specialisation:

- interworld navigation;
- structural memory;
- long-duration vessels;
- pressure-resistant silver paper and pale metal-card;
- preservation of a route after its landmarks disappear.

Contradiction: their chronicles praise perfect memory but contain the largest
number of retroactive corrections.

### Kh'Aryans — interpreters of dimensional form

Origin tradition: a green-star system whose name survives in several mutually
incompatible spellings.

Specialisation:

- multi-dimensional notation;
- conversion between scripts;
- navigation through Iriy and Interworld;
- layered green-black route cards;
- predictive diagrams.

Contradiction: their script is described as impossible for outsiders to
translate, followed by thousands of pages of confident outsider translations.

### Rasens — makers of inhabitable ground

Origin tradition: the system of Rasa.

Specialisation:

- settlement building;
- soil and climate adaptation;
- communal foundries;
- ochre and dark-red construction cards;
- negotiation between local materials and imported standards.

Contradiction: later military historians describe every repaired settlement as
evidence of conquest because the Rasens left the most visible material layer.

### Svyatorus — keepers of shared speech

Origin tradition: a blue-lit system associated with long-distance signals.

Specialisation:

- oral memory;
- treaty performance;
- signal networks;
- blue-white fibre ribbons;
- maintenance of meaning across dialects.

Contradiction: later purists cite the Svyatorus as proof of one unchanged
primordial language, although their entire craft concerns managing linguistic
change.

## 8. The racial doctrine inside the world

The four lineages are not biologically pure species. They are old fleet and
civic identities that later authorities convert into hereditary categories.

The conversion proceeds in five steps:

1. different vessels use different environmental adaptations;
2. adaptations produce visible traits in some generations;
3. registries record those traits for medical logistics;
4. genealogists treat the registry as a hierarchy of souls;
5. political movements claim modern populations are pure descendants.

The game must show this conversion as a historical fraud with real social
power. It must never reward racial purity, prohibit mixture or map a lineage to
a real ethnicity.

## 9. The Greys

### Origin

The beings later called **Greys** descend from several populations of compressed
Peklo worlds. Their bodies are adapted to low light, scarce dimensional space
and unstable identity boundaries.

They survive by temporarily copying external forms. Copying is originally a
medical and navigational necessity.

### The Grey accusation

Light chroniclers merge all Peklo refugees, mimetic organisms, captured
machines and political infiltrators into one category: the Greys.

They are accused of:

- lacking creative power;
- being neither male nor female;
- reproducing through mixture;
- stealing names and bodies;
- controlling other peoples;
- inventing religions of obedience.

The accusations contradict one another but become politically useful.

### What is true

- some Peklo organisms copy material forms;
- some Grey fleets infiltrated passage stations during the Great Assa;
- some copied identities became independent people;
- Light laboratories also used copying technology;
- the majority of those called Grey share no government or origin.

### Game role

The enemy “Grey” is initially a military classification. During the campaign,
the player discovers that applying the category causes unrelated beings to
physically acquire identical pale directive layers.

The stereotype manufactures the uniform enemy it claims to describe.

## 10. Serpent and lizard peoples

There is no single lizard race.

The label merges:

- the **Naga route-keepers**, adapted to flooded passages;
- the **Deep Scale houses**, living under high planetary pressure;
- survivors from Arey who moved beneath the surface;
- artificial maintenance bodies designed for tunnels;
- Koshchei military orders using egg-shaped life capsules;
- unrelated creatures depicted with serpentine forms in local mythology.

### The Naga route-keepers

They maintain water passages between settlements and preserve agreements by
periodic shedding: every shed scale records an expired version of a treaty.

### Deep Scale houses

They inhabit pressure chambers beneath planets and consider surface ownership
meaningless. Their conflicts concern access, load and broken drilling
agreements.

### Satirical conflict

Surface lecturers place all serpent images on one wall, draw arrows between
them and announce a unified reptilian conspiracy. The resulting propaganda
forces distinct serpent communities into an alliance they never previously
possessed.

## 11. Vaitmaras and Vaitmanas

### Vaitmara

A Vaitmara is a long-range interworld carrier capable of transporting several
clans, smaller vessels, seed ecologies and settlement machinery.

It is less a single ship than a moving district.

### Vaitmana

A Vaitmana is a smaller descent, inspection or courier craft. Its folding
geometry changes when crossing between dimensional conditions.

### Visual rule

Neither craft resembles a polished science-fiction saucer. They are enormous
layered travelling constructions assembled from replaceable route plates,
pressure ribs, woven memory surfaces and detachable local repairs.

### Satirical detail

Every surviving illustration is labelled “exact technical reconstruction”,
although no two show the same number of wings.

## 12. The decision to settle Midgard

Midgard is selected because it lies near several Golden Path intersections and
can support many imported ecologies.

The official chronicle says the Great Race discovered an empty world prepared
for them by the gods.

The settlement records show otherwise:

- serpent communities already maintain underground water routes;
- several human populations have arrived through earlier refugee transfers;
- local Nav contains memories of settlements older than the fleet;
- orbital agreements already exist around Mesyats and Lelya;
- the polar continent later called Daariya has inhabited southern margins.

The phrase “empty world” means “world absent from the fleet's property
registry”.

## 13. The First Landing

The four lineages do not land simultaneously.

1. Da'Aryan vessels establish stable polar approach routes.
2. Kh'Aryan navigators map dimensional weather.
3. Rasens adapt soil and build the first shared settlement rings.
4. Svyatorus connect separated camps through treaty songs and signals.

Later chronicles compress the process into one heroic landing beneath three
moons.

### The first dispute

The Deep Scale houses claim the landing damaged an ancient pressure route.
Fleet authorities answer that no such route exists because it is not visible
from orbit.

This unresolved dispute becomes the oldest debt of Midgard and the seed of the
later war around Antlan.

## 14. The first cosmic-age factions

### The Fleet Concord

Coalition of the four lineages. Publicly equal; administratively dominated by
whoever controls passage records.

### The Arleg Archive

Custodians of dangerous cross-world knowledge. Their secrecy contributes both
to stability and to conspiracy.

### The Passage Inspectors

Perun's travelling service. They begin as engineers and become a hereditary
priesthood in later ages.

### The Solar Covenant

Settlements operating under Dazhbog's orbital agreements. Membership is legal,
not biological, though later genealogists deny this.

### The Peklo Refugee Fleets

Multiple displaced populations using copied forms. They have no unified Grey
government.

### The Lower Passage Communities

Naga route-keepers, Deep Scale houses and other subterranean peoples. Their
temporary coalition is created by surface pressure.

### Mara's Terminators

Neutral custodians who end unstable forms and close exhausted passages. Both
sides accuse them of serving the enemy whenever they refuse resurrection.

## 15. First-age dramatic arc

### Movement 1 — Wonder

The audience sees the birth of Ingliya, the Golden Path, vast travelling
cities and four cultures cooperating across worlds.

### Movement 2 — Classification

Practical fleet registries become ancestry. Refugees from Peklo become one
Grey category. Distinct serpent peoples become one underground enemy.

### Movement 3 — Settlement

The Fleet arrives on inhabited Midgard while insisting it discovered an empty
world.

### Movement 4 — The first missing record

Evidence of earlier settlement is placed in the Arleg Archive because public
release might weaken the landing covenant.

### Movement 5 — Founding contradiction

The new civilisation defines itself as a people who never erase history while
its first legal act hides the history of those already present.

## 16. First-age gameable conflicts

- escort four differently constructed fleet sections through unstable Golden
  Path passages;
- distinguish actual Peklo infiltrators from refugees given copied bodies;
- negotiate a route with Naga keepers while propaganda marks them as one
  lizard army;
- prevent a medical registry from becoming hereditary law;
- decide whether to open Chernobog's unsafe knowledge fragments;
- close a dying passage with Mara's help while settlers call closure sabotage;
- recover the pre-landing pressure agreement beneath Daariya;
- witness the first political use of “grandchildren of Dazhbog”.

## 17. Visual foundation

The first age combines cosmic scale with the established cardboard material:

- stars are punched openings through layered black-blue paper;
- Golden Paths are long removable metallic-paper route strips;
- worlds are nested discs with independent atmosphere, memory and ownership
  layers;
- Vaitmaras are travelling punchboard districts, not plastic ships;
- gods are larger directional assemblies made of the same material laws as
  other beings;
- Grey copied forms use pale replaceable overlays over varied underlying
  bodies;
- serpent communities use warm clay paper, pressure stone and water-silver
  joints;
- Mara uses clean finished edges and deliberately absent continuation tabs.

Exact top-down projection remains mandatory for gameplay objects. Narrative
concept art may show scale diagrams, but never generic glossy space fantasy.

## 18. Canon boundaries

Canon inside the fictional world:

- Ra-M-Ha and Ingliya are the oldest surviving explanation, not confirmed
  objective truth;
- the Golden Path and multiple worlds materially exist;
- the Great Assa occurred, but its unified Light-versus-Dark account is
  propaganda;
- the four lineages are civic/fleet cultures, not pure biological races;
- gods are real powerful beings, while their genealogy remains disputed;
- Peklo populations and copied bodies exist; the unified Grey race does not;
- many serpent peoples exist; the universal lizard conspiracy does not;
- Midgard was inhabited before the Great Race arrived;
- the first civilisation knowingly concealed part of that evidence.

Not canon:

- supremacy of a white or Indo-Aryan race;
- real modern peoples as descendants of cosmic lineages;
- real religions or ethnic groups as Grey agents;
- racial mixture as corruption;
- the official Fleet Chronicle as infallible history.

The next world document begins with the settlement of Daariya under three
moons. It must cover:

- the four rivers and four districts;
- coexistence and conflict with Lower Passage communities;
- construction of Asgard Iriysky;
- Perun's visit;
- Dazhbog's lunar administration;
- occupation of Lelya by the Koshchei;
- Spas's warning and the Great Migration;
- destruction of Lelya and the first flood.
